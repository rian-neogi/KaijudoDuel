# NPC Backstories

This document covers every NPC currently present in Emberglen, followed by the major off-screen characters who drive the campaign.

## Mira — Keeper of Unwanted Memories

**Civilization:** Darkness  
**Signature card:** Zagaan, Knight of Darkness  
**Gameplay identity:** Graveyard value, attrition, and patient removal

Mira grew up above Emberglen's old memorial hall, where damaged cards were catalogued instead of discarded. She learned early that Darkness cards are not inherently cruel; many simply preserve the thoughts and losses that other civilizations would prefer to bury. Her talent is hearing faint echoes in cards everyone else believes are blank.

Years ago, Mira found the broken-circle mark on a group of fragments sent from a distant archive. Rowan asked her to keep the discovery quiet while he investigated. She agreed, but she has regretted that secrecy ever since. During the festival attack, she recognizes the same mark immediately and becomes one of the player's three essential witnesses.

Mira speaks calmly and rarely volunteers more than she knows is useful. Her apparent gloom hides a dry sense of humor and a fierce refusal to let anyone's history be erased. Repeated duels teach her to trust the player with Zagaan's echo rather than guarding every dangerous memory alone.

In Act II, Gloam Quarry tests her hardest belief: whether painful memories always deserve preservation. Her arc ends when she accepts that remembering something does not require being ruled by it.

## Marin — Reader of Currents

**Civilization:** Water  
**Signature card:** Aqua Sniper  
**Gameplay identity:** Information, hand control, and precise tempo

Marin is the daughter of canal surveyors who map the underground streams feeding Emberglen. She thinks in routes, probabilities, and changing currents. To her, a duel is a problem that becomes solvable once enough information has been revealed.

She came to the festival to study how Water techniques could predict cross-civilization matchups. The Fading embarrasses her because none of her models anticipated it. She initially responds by becoming even more analytical, reviewing every card position and witness statement instead of admitting she is frightened.

Marin respects opponents who change plans halfway through a duel. The player's repeated victories force her to distinguish genuine foresight from the illusion of control. Giving up Aqua Sniper is meaningful because it represents her willingness to let another duelist make choices she cannot predict.

Marin is also preparing for the Grand Championship.

At Glasswater Port in Act II, Marin encounters perfect counterfeit predictions derived from stolen memories. She must choose between flawless information and the uncertainty that allows people to grow.

## Rook — The Patient Wall

**Civilization:** Nature  
**Signature card:** Roaring Great-Horn  
**Gameplay identity:** Mana growth followed by overwhelming creatures

Rook helps maintain the old road and the timber bridges surrounding Emberglen. He earned his nickname by approaching every task like a fortress piece: choose a position, make it secure, and move only when the whole structure can support the next step.

He was once an impatient young duelist who spent every resource as soon as he drew it. A disastrous wilderness match stranded his team after he ignored warnings from his creatures. Since then, he has built decks around steady growth and refuses to mistake speed for strength.

Rook is blunt, reliable, and more comfortable repairing a fence than discussing fear. When the town's cards fade, he focuses on keeping paths open and pretends that practical work is enough. Dueling the player reminds him that preparation matters because it protects people, not because perfect preparation is possible.

His bond with Roaring Great-Horn represents strength accumulated responsibly. In Act II, he becomes a natural protector for refugees crossing Rootmaze, but must learn to move before every risk can be eliminated.

## Aurelia — The Festival Adjudicator

**Civilization:** Light  
**Signature card:** Hanusa, Radiance Elemental  
**Gameplay identity:** Defensive guardians, control, and shield pressure

Aurelia trained at Sunspire Cloister before moving to Emberglen as a duel judge. She believes rules create a safe space in which courage can matter. Her formal manner comes from years of settling disputes between duelists who interpreted honor very differently.

Rowan trusted Aurelia to supervise the Festival of Five Civilizations. When the lights fail, she sees the blank cards turn toward the northern bridge and watches Rowan pursue them. She blames herself for failing to stop him, which is why her clue is delivered as a precise report rather than a confession of worry.

Aurelia is generous but can become inflexible when afraid. Her duels test whether the player can attack intelligently through a disciplined defense. Each rematch gradually replaces judgment with mutual respect, culminating in her sharing Hanusa's echo.

Aurelia is the first Crest holder, but she does not battle the player until the player is ready for the first crest battle. She also adjudicates the Grand Champsionship when it happens.

When Sunspire's guardians turn on one another in Act II, Aurelia confronts the danger in treating rules as more important than the people they protect. Her later leadership is gentler and more willing to listen.

## Flint — Arena Mechanic and Firebrand

**Civilization:** Fire  
**Signature card:** Astrocomet Dragon  
**Gameplay identity:** Fast pressure, power boosts, and explosive finishers

Flint repairs Emberglen's arena lights, card tables, and festival machinery. He also enters nearly every local tournament, partly because he loves dueling and partly because broken equipment is easiest to understand from the player's side of the table.

He saw the masked stranger beside the arena's power junction moments before the blackout. Flint rushed the figure and was thrown back by a card with no name or civilization mark. His pride makes him emphasize what he saw rather than how completely he was outmatched.

Flint is loud, competitive, and quick to make a friend out of anyone willing to accept a rematch. Beneath that confidence is a fear of being useless when speed and force do not solve the problem. The player earns Astrocomet Dragon by surviving his aggression and showing that boldness works best when paired with judgment.

Flint is also preparing to compete in the Grand Championship.

At Cinderrail Foundry, Flint discovers machines built from the same components he once repaired for an unknown buyer. His Act II arc turns guilt into action as he dismantles the Curator's forced-duel engines.

## Nyx — The Grave Listener

**Civilization:** Darkness  
**Signature card:** Deathliger, Lion of Chaos  
**Gameplay identity:** Sacrifice, graveyard recursion, and powerful late threats

Nyx is a wandering duelist who arrived in Emberglen shortly before the festival. They practice an old Darkness discipline called grave listening: reconstructing a duel's emotional shape from the cards that were destroyed or discarded.

Unlike Mira, who preserves written records, Nyx trusts instinct and ritual. This makes the two natural rivals. Mira thinks Nyx is careless with dangerous memories; Nyx thinks Mira mistakes cataloguing for understanding.

Nyx felt the Fading as a sudden silence in the graveyard zone. The experience terrified them, although they describe it poetically enough that most townspeople never notice. Their severe manner softens during repeated duels, especially when the player demonstrates that sacrifice can create new possibilities rather than merely celebrate loss.

Nyx is going to be competing for the Grand Championships alongside the player.

The Curator's offer at Gloam Quarry is personally tempting: Nyx could erase the echoes of every defeat they failed to prevent. Rejecting that offer becomes their defining choice.

## Tidal — The Retired Tideguard

**Civilization:** Water  
**Signature card:** King Depthcon  
**Gameplay identity:** Card advantage, deep defenses, and patient finishers

Tidal once served in the Tideguard, escorting card shipments through river routes where smugglers and rogue creatures were common. An injury ended that career, and Mercer persuaded him to settle in Emberglen as a caravan guard.

He teaches patience because he has seen hurried decisions turn small dangers into disasters. Where Marin wants to calculate every future current, Tidal watches, waits, and keeps enough resources to survive being wrong.

Tidal's quietness can be mistaken for detachment. In truth, he notices nearly everyone in town and positions himself near the southern road during the crisis so no frightened resident has to leave alone. His rematches become increasingly demanding tests of endurance, and King Depthcon is his acknowledgment that the player can carry responsibility without rushing it.

At Glasswater Port, Tidal clashes with Marin over whether the stolen prediction system should be destroyed or studied. Their eventual cooperation combines her curiosity with his caution.

## Briar — Warden of the Living Paths

**Civilization:** Nature  
**Signature card:** Deathblade Beetle  
**Gameplay identity:** Mana acceleration, resilient creatures, and natural traps

Briar tends the groves and creature paths west of Emberglen. She treats summoned creatures as visitors with their own needs rather than tools that disappear when a duel ends. Many of the town's younger duelists first learn creature care from her.

She found several forest creatures wandering without recognition after their card echoes faded. The sight makes her unusually angry, and she channels that anger into relentless training. Her friendliness returns once the player proves willing to build a deck around cooperation rather than raw ownership.

Briar and Rook share a Nature philosophy but express it differently. Rook cultivates strength through structure; Briar trusts adaptation and the messy intelligence of living systems. Their arguments are affectionate and constant.

In Rootmaze, Briar becomes the first person able to communicate with creatures severed from their cards. Her bond with Deathblade Beetle helps establish that an echo can survive outside its original physical card for a short time.

## Mercer — Keeper of the Practical Things

**Role:** Shopkeeper and caravan organizer  
**Gameplay identity:** Card shop, gold sink, and campaign supply network

Mercer inherited a small card stall and expanded it by traveling between isolated towns. He cannot duel well and has no desire to pretend otherwise. His skill is remembering what people need, what they can afford, and which two strangers should be introduced to each other.

Years of trade taught Mercer that cards with damaged ink can still carry valuable echoes. After the festival, he collects blank fragments rather than throwing them away and keeps the player's card supply moving. His cheerful prices and sales patter are genuine, but so is his awareness that the Curator will follow any restored echo.

Mercer once transported sealed archive equipment for Rowan and the Curator without knowing its purpose. The machinery later used at Cinderrail bears his old caravan mark. He carries guilt over this until Flint points out that responsibility lies in what they do after learning the truth.

In Act II, Mercer becomes a traveling shopkeeper whose stock expands with every repaired route. In Act III, his mundane logistics—food, blank cards, safe roads, and messages—are what allow the grand alliance to function.

## The Veiled One — An Empty Rival

**Civilizations:** All five  
**Signature card:** Urth, Purifying Elemental  
**Gameplay identity:** Flexible answers, efficient control, and boss-level adaptation

The Veiled One is not a human being. It is a body assembled from blank fragments, animated by a copied portion of the Curator's memory. Its cloak and mask conceal the absence of a face, while its voice is produced from stolen pieces of past challengers.

The shell exists to test whether Emberglen can create stable echoes after the Fading. If the town fails, the Curator loses nothing. If it succeeds, the Veiled One draws the restored resonance to the bridge and measures the player directly.

Its five-civilization deck is an early prototype of the Hollow Deck. Unlike the final machine, it still requires stable borrowed cards such as Urth. This is why it is genuinely stronger and more flexible than the local duelists but can still be defeated by a deck with a coherent identity.

After defeat, the shell disintegrates and the Curator speaks through its last intact fragment. The Veiled One has no redemption arc of its own, but hollow replicas in Act III can reuse its unsettling visual language.

## Rowan — The Missing Mentor

**Role:** Mentor, echo scholar, and former archive researcher

Rowan taught the player to ask what a deck is trying to express before deciding whether it is strong. He travels with a deliberately modest deck and changes it often, believing dependence on one signature card can turn affection into possession.

He and the Curator once studied how echoes preserve card rules across generations. Their partnership ended after an archive experiment amplified a painful memory and caused a destructive resonance cascade. Rowan concluded that the research needed consent and living bonds. The Curator concluded that memory itself was the flaw.

Rowan has spent years quietly dismantling their old work. He chose Emberglen because its five-civilization festival generated a balanced resonance capable of hiding the player's starter deck from the Curator. When the Fading begins, he follows the signal to keep it away from the town and is captured.

His weakness is secrecy. Even when trying to protect others, Rowan withholds choices they deserve to make. Acts II and III force him to treat the player as a partner rather than a student.

## The Curator — Architect of Silence

**Role:** Main antagonist and creator of the Hollow Deck

The Curator's original name has been removed from every surviving record, partly by his own hand. He was a gifted archivist who could hear contradictions between layers of a card's echo. He began as a preservationist, rescuing cards whose memories were close to collapse.

The archive accident convinced him that personal attachment makes all echoes unstable. He now believes a perfectly ordered, memoryless system would prevent grief, cheating, corruption, and civilization conflict. He does not describe his victims as stolen from; he describes them as relieved of dangerous burdens.

His tragedy is that he still acts from an archivist's desire to save things. He simply no longer accepts that a preserved object without relationships may not be meaningfully alive. The player threatens him because their collection becomes stronger through voluntary gifts, directly contradicting the premise of the Hollow Deck.

The Curator should remain calm and exact even when defeated. His eventual realization comes from watching imperfect echoes cooperate in a way his flawless copies cannot reproduce.
