# World Physics: Cards, Resonance, and the Confluence

## Purpose of this document

This file defines the metaphysical rules shared by the overworld, duels, card
rewards, shards, Resonance Seals, and the Hollow storyline. It is the canonical
reference when another story note uses terms such as **resonance**, **echo**,
**current**, **shard**, **Seal**, **Fading**, or **Confluence**.

These rules should make the setting feel consistent without turning every scene
into a scientific lecture. Most residents understand the practical rules in the
same way that ordinary people understand weather, electricity, or medicine.
Scholars disagree about origins and fine details, but repeatable effects have
repeatable limits.

## The two layers of the world

The world consists of ordinary matter and a second, interacting layer called
the **resonant field**.

Ordinary matter follows familiar physical rules. Stone has weight, fire needs
fuel, trains need engines, a broken bridge must be repaired, and a person cannot
teleport merely by wishing. Resonance can carry patterns through matter and can
temporarily embody a card's creature or spell, but it does not abolish distance,
energy cost, structural strength, or bodily danger.

The resonant field responds to three things:

1. A stable pattern, such as a card's identity and rules
2. A compatible source of resonance
3. A relationship capable of giving that pattern context

A printed card provides the pattern. A duel field and its mana provide the
source. The duelist's practiced relationship with the card supplies context.
All three are necessary for a clear, stable manifestation.

Resonance is therefore neither a soul nor an ordinary fuel. It is closer to the
way a musical instrument, a room, and a performer together produce a particular
sound. The instrument can be copied, but two performances still acquire
different histories.

## The five civilizations

Light, Water, Darkness, Fire, and Nature are five stable modes of resonance.
They are called civilizations because each mode carries coherent creatures,
cultures, places, and ways of acting—not merely a color or elemental spell list.

The civilizations do not occupy ordinary neighboring countries on the
overworld map. They are persistent domains within the resonant layer. A card is
a safe address and agreement through which one of their patterns can answer in
the material world. A duel manifestation does not abduct a flesh-and-blood
creature from a distant home; it gives local form to a creature pattern capable
of memory, response, and limited agency.

Each mode favors a different kind of relationship:

| Civilization | Stable resonance favors | Typical instability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | Duty, protection, structure, and promises kept | Rigidity and authority without listening |
| Water | Information, adaptation, motion, and chosen change | Detachment and knowledge taken without consent |
| Darkness | Memory, loss, consequence, and transformation | Obsession, erasure, or sacrifice without purpose |
| Fire | Intention, courage, momentum, and decisive change | Waste, escalation, and force without preparation |
| Nature | Growth, interdependence, inheritance, and renewal | Stagnation disguised as tradition or growth without limits |

No civilization is morally good or evil. A Light ward can protect or imprison;
a Darkness echo can preserve grief or exploit it. The story's conflicts arise
from how people use a mode, not from the mode itself.

## What a card is

A duel card is a physical object made from ordinary card stock, resonant inks,
and a licensed or inherited **master impression**. The master impression defines
the card's shared name, civilization, cost, race, power, and rules. Two copies
of the same named card begin from the same impression but are not metaphysically
identical.

Every usable card has three nested layers:

- **The frame:** The physical card, ink, and conductive material
- **The core pattern:** The reproducible identity and rules derived from the
  master impression
- **The echo:** The history accumulated by this particular copy through use,
  care, risk, transfer, and relationships

The core pattern lets a newly made card function. The echo lets it answer with
clarity and individuality. Over time the distinction becomes difficult to see,
because a strong echo reinforces the core pattern while a damaged echo can make
the printed name, art, and rules lose definition.

Cards can be printed, repaired, inherited, traded, or gifted. They are not
created from nothing during a shop transaction. The game's unlimited merchant
stock is a gameplay abstraction for an ongoing supply of ordinary copies.

## Echoes

An **echo** is the card's persistent relational history. It includes impressions
of past decisions and bonds, but it is not a complete recording of every duel
and cannot reproduce a duelist's whole mind.

Echoes obey the following rules:

- Repeated use strengthens an echo only when the card remains part of meaningful
  decisions. Grinding empty motions produces noise, not a deep bond.
- Winning is not required. A difficult loss can leave a stronger echo than an
  effortless victory.
- Care, deck-building choices, and voluntary transfer can affect an echo outside
  a formal duel.
- Two copies of the same card can develop very different echoes while retaining
  identical legal rules.
- An echo may contain emotional impressions, but reading one requires skill and
  interpretation. It is not reliable mind reading.
- An echo can be copied imperfectly, displaced temporarily, or stripped from its
  physical frame. Each operation loses context and increases instability.
- An echo is not a human soul. Resonance cannot resurrect a dead person or make
  an exact, morally equivalent replacement of them.

### Signature cards

A **signature card** is a particular copy whose echo has become strongly linked
to a duelist's characteristic decisions. It does not become stronger by
secretly changing its printed rules. Its stability, clarity, and responsiveness
make the duelist better able to express the strategy already permitted by those
rules.

This is why a hollow replica can own the same card list yet play like a
caricature. It has copied the legal pattern without the relationship that taught
the original duelist when to take risks, wait, sacrifice, or change plans.

### Card rewards and the player's collection

When an NPC awards a card, the transfer is voluntary. Depending on the character,
they may give the player a practiced copy, a newly restored copy from the same
line, or a card deliberately chosen from their reserve collection.

The act of willing transfer provides the first stable link to the player. It
does not steal the NPC's signature bond or duplicate their memories. The player
must still build a distinct echo with the received copy.

The player's collection resists the Fading unusually well because it is built
from these acknowledged transfers rather than forced extraction. This is a
story property of the collection loop, not a claim that ownership alone creates
consent.

## What happens during a duel

A duel uses a bounded resonance field produced by a sanctioned table, portable
duel focus, or permanent venue. The field gives abstract card zones a stable
physical role and prevents manifested force from behaving like uncontrolled
ordinary violence.

### The deck and hand

The deck is the set of patterns a duelist has prepared to sustain together.
Drawing a card brings one pattern into immediate attention. A card in hand is
available but not yet committed to a field role.

Deck construction matters metaphysically as well as strategically. Patterns
that share civilizations, races, or a coherent plan reinforce one another.
Five unrelated powerful cards do not automatically produce a stable five-color
deck.

### Mana

Charging a card as mana commits its pattern to stabilizing the field instead of
manifesting that card directly. Each charged card forms a small resonant channel
aligned to its civilization.

Tapping mana releases the channel's safely available potential for the current
cycle. Untapping does not create new energy from nothing; it represents the
field settling and the duelist re-establishing control. A card's mana cost is
the field load required to embody its effect without collapse.

Civilization requirements exist because raw capacity and correct alignment are
different things. A large amount of Fire-aligned mana cannot safely substitute
for the first Water alignment needed by a Water pattern.

Mana used inside a duel is temporary field organization. It is not a universal
currency or an effortless power source for cities. Large civic resonance
machines still require material conductors, maintenance, cooling, and ordinary
energy.

### Creatures and spells

Playing a creature produces a temporary embodied echo inside the duel boundary.
It has presence and can respond to its duelist, but the field limits how far it
can travel and how its force affects observers.

Playing a spell forms a shorter-lived event pattern. The visible fire, water,
light, shadow, or growth is resonantly embodied and collapses when the rule has
resolved. It can interact powerfully with duel manifestations without behaving
like an unrestricted weapon outside the field.

### Battle and the graveyard

When a creature loses a battle or is destroyed by a rule, its manifestation
collapses and the physical card moves to the graveyard zone. The creature has
not been killed as a biological individual. The graveyard represents patterns
that remain part of the duel's history but no longer have an active body.

Darkness strategies interact strongly with the graveyard because Darkness
resonance preserves consequence and transformation. Other civilizations can
also recover cards when their rules provide a stable method.

### Shields and direct attacks

Shield cards form layered wards between a duelist and the opposing field.
Keeping their identities face down preserves unrealized possibilities, which is
part of what gives the ward strength. Breaking a shield collapses one layer and
returns its card to immediate attention. A shield trigger is a pattern designed
to answer during that collapse.

A successful direct attack does not physically maul the losing duelist. It
breaks their remaining control of the bounded field and ends the match. Fatigue,
shock, or minor feedback can occur, which is why sanctioned equipment and
referees still matter.

### Tapping, blocking, and power

Tapping a creature represents committing its manifestation to a major action.
The turned card records that commitment until the next recovery cycle. A
blocker is a pattern stable enough to interpose itself within the field's attack
path.

Printed power measures how much opposing manifested force the creature can
overcome before its own embodiment collapses. It is not a complete measurement
of intelligence, narrative importance, or physical size.

### Evolution

An evolution card overlays a compatible active creature pattern with a more
complex lineage. The original manifestation provides the living anchor; the
evolution supplies a stronger form and rule set. When the evolution collapses,
all physical cards in that stack leave the battle zone together because the
shared embodiment has ended.

## Natural resonance currents

Resonance is not evenly distributed through the landscape. It travels along
**currents** shaped by geology, water, living systems, old settlements, and
repeated card use. Currents are not glowing underground pipes. They are broad
regions where one mode propagates more easily and can be measured through
specialized stone, glass, ink, plants, waterworks, or metal instruments.

Currents can cross without blending completely. Engineers describe them as
braids: close enough to affect one another, distinct enough to retain alignment.
This distinction is essential. Healthy cooperation lets different modes respond
together; forced homogenization destroys the differences that make them useful.

Roads often follow calm spaces between currents. Sanctuaries form where one
current becomes unusually clear. Towns such as Glasswater and Cinderrail build
infrastructure around predictable local behavior, but resonance never replaces
ordinary engineering.

## The Confluence

**The Confluence is a natural phenomenon, not the name of a town.** It is the
largest known place in the frontier where all five civilization currents pass
close together while remaining individually measurable.

The currents do not originate there, and the Confluence does not control all
resonance in the world. It is important because a signal introduced at the
junction can be translated into all five alignments with relatively little
loss. The same property also makes a corrupted broadcast unusually dangerous.

### Why the roads meet there

The stable gaps between the five currents create natural travel corridors. Old
roads followed those corridors long before scholars could explain them, so
their junction developed into a strategic checkpoint. The Archive was later
built beyond it because researchers could compare all five modes without moving
their instruments between distant sanctuaries.

### Dragon Keep

Dragon Keep is the castle built above and around the Confluence. It began as a
road fortress, not a royal palace. Its foundations anchor observation chambers,
current separators, and the relay mast while its gates control ordinary travel
between the inner and outer road networks.

The Keep does not magically open roads. Its wardens inspect physical routes,
maintain signal stations, and operate Circuit checkpoints. Story progression at
the Keep represents repaired infrastructure and institutional authorization,
not mountains vanishing when a Crest is won.

### Why it is called Dragon Keep

Dragon echoes have unusually high **resonance inertia**: once clearly embodied,
they resist distortion and demand a large, coherent field to redirect. Dragons
are not a sixth civilization; several civilizations contain Dragon lineages,
each aligned in its own way.

During the earliest Confluence surveys, ordinary test patterns produced readings
too faint to separate from background noise. Dragon-linked cards provided a
strong reference signal. If the junction could carry a Dragon manifestation
without bleeding its alignment into neighboring currents, engineers knew the
separators were working.

An early surge nearly collapsed the five currents into one blank interference
pattern. Five Dragon-linked reference cards held their alignments long enough
for the first wardens to close the relay. Stone dragons on the Keep's buttresses
commemorate that event. The castle took its present name from those stabilizing
echoes, not from a monarch or a population of tame physical dragons.

### Kestrel's Dragon deck

Kestrel continues the wardens' calibration tradition with a mono-Fire Dragon
deck. Fire Dragons place an especially loud, fast-changing load on the relay,
making them useful for detecting weak separators. Smaller Dragonoids and Fire
Birds prepare the field before Bolmeteus Steel Dragon and Uberdragon Bajula
apply the true stress test.

This is also the flaw the Chief Collector exploits. The Dragon signal is so
strong that a carefully shaped false pattern can hide beneath it. Kestrel knows
how to change routes and apply pressure, but the crisis forces him to study the
older baseline readings he habitually replaced.

### The Confluence Crest

The fifth Circuit award remains the **Confluence Crest** even though its location
is Dragon Keep. Its three interlocking pieces represent distinct currents held
in a coherent relationship. Winning it proves that a challenger can support a
powerful plan through preparation and pressure without mistaking raw strength
for stability.

The Crest is a sporting credential, not a component of the Confluence machinery.
Like other long-used ceremonial objects, it can carry a modest echo and relay a
prepared message, but possessing it does not grant control over the currents.

## Resonance Seals

Each civilization has one frontier sanctuary built around an exceptionally
clear section of its current:

| Civilization | Sanctuary region | Seal records |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | Sunspire Cloister | Protection sustained by judgment and living testimony |
| Water | Glasswater Port | Information used with consent and capacity to adapt |
| Darkness | Gloam Quarry | Memory connected to consequence, grief, and chosen change |
| Fire | Cinderrail Foundry | Decisive force supported by preparation and responsibility |
| Nature | Rootmaze Commons | Growth sustained through interdependence and stewardship |

A **Resonance Seal** is a calibration standard, not a padlock, battery, or magic
gem. It records the healthy reference pattern produced when a community and its
current remain in a stable relationship. Physical Seal mechanisms protect and
measure that reference, while living community practices keep it meaningful.

A damaged Seal does not switch off its civilization. It makes corruption harder
to distinguish from legitimate variation. Restoring a Seal requires repairing
both its machinery or sanctuary and the local breach of trust that made the
false pattern convincing.

The five Seals together provide a trustworthy comparison across all currents.
The Curator needs their patterns to make the Hollow Deck's blank broadcast look
valid in every alignment. The Crests are never substitutes for the Seals.

## Shards

A **shard** is a small piece of resonance-sensitive mineral, glass, ceramic, or
archive prism that retains a narrow calibration pattern. Most shards form where
repeated use of related cards has impressed one race or civilization strongly
enough into a receptive material. Others are fragments of old trade or archive
instruments.

A shard is named for its clearest stable pattern, such as **Ghost Shard**,
**Liquid People Shard**, or **Dragonoid Shard**. A race shard is more specific
than a civilization shard. Names describe alignment, not the material from which
the shard was cut.

Shards have strict limits:

- They contain no complete card rules and cannot be placed in a deck.
- They do not contain a trapped creature, person, or complete personal memory.
- Finding a shard does not imply that a creature was killed to produce it.
- They provide a calibration reference, not an unlimited source of resonance.
- A shard can help identify, authenticate, or repair compatible dormant cards,
  but cannot create arbitrary cards from nothing.

### Why Mercer wants shards

Mercer owns boxes of faded, misregistered, and difficult-to-authenticate cards
obtained through years of trade. His restoration bench can recover them only
when it has a trustworthy race or civilization reference.

Giving Mercer a shard lets him tune the bench, verify compatible stock, and
identify suppliers whose cards match that pattern. The shard remains installed
as a reference rather than being consumed. His newly available cards come from
repaired inventory and reopened trade, which the game represents as expanded
shop stock.

This is why a Beast Folk Shard unlocks a family of Beast Folk cards instead of
granting the player one random card. It is also why Mercer's stock remains
centralized in Emberglen even when the shard was found in another region.

## The Fading

The **Fading** is forced dephasing between physical cards and their echoes. The
Curator's signal does not burn the card. It progressively removes relational
context, then destabilizes the core pattern that keeps resonant ink and artwork
coherent.

Stages of the Fading are:

1. A card feels distant or responds slowly during manifestation.
2. Fine art, names, and remembered emotional details become inconsistent.
3. Rules and civilization alignment lose clarity.
4. The echo separates, leaving a pale blank fragment.

A blank fragment is still physical card stock but cannot answer a duelist. It
may be restored if its displaced echo survives and a trustworthy relationship
can guide it home. Merely repainting the name does not recreate the lost echo.

Rootmaze establishes that a displaced creature echo can remain briefly embodied
outside its original card when supported by a strong local current and living
bonds. This is temporary survival, not proof that echoes no longer need anchors.

## The Hollow Deck

The Hollow Deck preserves reproducible card structure while removing provenance,
contradiction, and voluntary relationship. It can copy what a card does more
easily than why a duelist chooses to do it.

Forced echoes are unstable because extraction severs the context that organized
them. The Hollow Deck compensates through machinery and the five stolen Seal
patterns. If completed, it could make its blank reference appear normal to all
five currents and broadcast the Fading through the Confluence relay.

The Curator calls this preservation because legal patterns would remain orderly
and predictable. The central moral and physical flaw is the same: a relationship
cannot remain itself after every participant and history has been removed.

The Hollow Deck cannot create true people, resurrect the dead, or produce
perfect minds from card traces. Its replicas are bounded constructs assembled
from partial impressions. Their accuracy can be emotionally convincing without
making them continuous with the people they imitate.

## Technology and everyday life

Most technology is ordinary engineering enhanced by carefully bounded resonance:

- Cinderrail uses furnaces and mechanical rail power, with resonance for control
  and monitoring rather than free energy.
- Glasswater uses pumps, gates, and cisterns whose Water-aligned instruments
  predict and regulate flow.
- Stormbreak uses physical cables, counterweights, shelters, and signal towers;
  resonance coordinates them but does not create walkable air.
- Dragon Keep uses stone separators, conductive metal, archive prisms, and a
  relay mast to compare and transmit aligned signals.
- Duel equipment confines manifestations so ordinary towns can safely host
  matches.

Devices wear out. Conductors heat, glass clouds, inks decay, living calibration
gardens need care, and operators make mistakes. A resonant solution should
always have material maintenance and a human decision attached to it.

## Hard limits for future stories

To keep the world coherent, future quests should respect these limits:

- Resonance cannot resurrect a dead person or prove that a replica is the same
  person.
- Echo reading is partial and interpretive, never effortless omniscience.
- Consent improves relational stability but is not a spell that guarantees
  safety, truth, or victory.
- A strong emotion alone cannot rewrite a legal card rule during an ordinary
  sanctioned duel.
- Creature and spell manifestations are bounded by duel fields unless a major
  malfunction or sanctuary event explicitly supplies another anchor.
- Resonance does not create limitless energy, food, money, cards, or materials.
- Roads open through repair and authorization, not because a Crest rearranges
  geography.
- Crests are Circuit credentials. Seals are civilization calibration standards.
  Shards are narrow technical references. These objects are not interchangeable.
- The Confluence is a junction and translator, not the source of all magic or a
  single switch that can permanently disable a civilization.
- Dragons have high resonance inertia, not automatic moral authority or immunity
  to card rules.
- The five civilizations remain distinct even when cooperating. Healthy unity
  is a braid, not a blend into a sixth colorless mode.

## Terminology summary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Resonance | The interaction between a stable pattern, a compatible field, and a relationship |
| Civilization current | One of five landscape-scale modes through which aligned resonance propagates |
| Core pattern | A card's reproducible legal identity and rules |
| Echo | The persistent relational history of a particular card copy |
| Signature card | A copy with an especially strong link to one duelist's characteristic decisions |
| Manifestation | A temporary creature or spell embodiment formed inside a bounded field |
| Mana | Card patterns committed to stabilizing and powering the current duel field |
| Graveyard | The duel role holding patterns whose active manifestations have collapsed |
| Confluence | The natural five-current junction beneath Dragon Keep |
| Dragon Keep | The castle, relay checkpoint, and fifth Crest location built over the Confluence |
| Confluence Crest | Kestrel's Circuit credential, named for the junction rather than the castle |
| Resonance Seal | A sanctuary's trusted calibration standard for one civilization current |
| Shard | A narrow race or civilization calibration pattern retained in receptive material |
| Fading | Forced separation of cards from their echoes and eventually their core patterns |
| Hollow Deck | An artificial deck that preserves copied function while erasing relational identity |
