# Optional Towns

## Design rules

These settlements exist to make the world feel inhabited beyond the Fivefold Circuit and the Hollow investigation. They are explorable side-road destinations, not disguised campaign requirements.

- No optional town contains an Arena Crest, Resonance Seal, required Hollow clue, or mandatory Archive ally.
- Entering or completing one never blocks another route.
- Their quests award cards, gold, cosmetics, shop stock, or convenient services rather than story-critical powers.
- Duelists use the normal four-victory reward structure and may have stronger rematch decks.
- Town dialogue may react to major world events, but resolving the Hollow crisis never depends on these reactions.
- During Act III, every town keeps its essential services available. Blank-zone effects can change presentation and dialogue without removing player conveniences.

The first three towns become reachable with the first open-world tier. The final three become reachable after Dragon Keep's Confluence gate opens.

## First-tier towns

### Lilyreed Village

**Side road:** West of Glasswater Port  
**Visual identity:** A fishing village built on painted stilts, joined by rope walkways and small hand-pulled ferries  
**Common decks:** Water/Nature hand filtering, mana recovery, blockers, and creature swapping

Lilyreed follows the tide rather than a clock. Shops open when their owners' boats return, the central duel platform rises and falls with the water, and nearly every family maintains a different shortcut through the reed beds.

Its self-contained story concerns the annual Lantern Regatta. Two ferry crews each claim that the other copied a winning route. The player duels both crews, finds that they learned the route from the same retired navigator, and helps turn the dispute into a cooperative relay race. Nothing discovered here relates to the Curator.

Gameplay uses narrow docks, moving ferries, and optional trainer circuits. The local trader exchanges excess common cards for rotating Water and Nature stock, giving duplicate cards a modest use without becoming the best source of mandatory staples.

**Anchor NPCs:**

- **Mara Pike, ferrymaster:** Water/Nature tempo; organizes the Regatta rematch ladder.
- **Old Fen, retired navigator:** Offers deck advice and trades basic search cards.
- **Tib and Tobb, rival siblings:** Separate Water and Nature decks.

### Honeyreach

**Side road:** South of Rootmaze Commons  
**Visual identity:** Terraced orchards, oversized beehive roofs, flower-lined paths, and amber glass windows  
**Common decks:** Nature/Light creature swarms, mana growth, blockers, and board-wide attack effects

Honeyreach is cheerful, prosperous, and intensely competitive about fruit. Its harvest festival has more duel brackets than food categories because every orchard insists that a tournament is the fairest way to choose the year's parade order.

The local story is a low-stakes rivalry between the hill and valley orchards. The player may represent either side first, but eventually learns that both are secretly using cuttings from the same beloved old tree. A final exhibition duel turns the argument into a shared festival tradition.

The town sells beginner-friendly Nature and Light cards and hosts rotating deck-size challenges. Rewards emphasize flexible support cards, gold, and orchard-themed card sleeves rather than unique progression items.

**Anchor NPCs:**

- **Jessa Bloom, festival marshal:** Light/Nature swarm deck and town tournament registration.
- **Corren Vale, hill grower:** Nature acceleration into Beast Folk evolutions.
- **Min Honey, beekeeper:** Light blockers that become attackers after the board is established.

### Clayhearth

**Side road:** East of Cinderrail Foundry  
**Visual identity:** Round brick homes, colored chimney caps, open pottery yards, and a warm communal kiln  
**Common decks:** Fire/Light board control, careful removal, blockers, and supported evolution attacks

Clayhearth uses heat from the Cinderrail line but has no role in its arena or Resonance Seal. The town is known for durable cookware, painted duel tokens, and residents who can discuss glaze chemistry for much longer than any visitor expects.

Its side story follows an argument over the design of a new communal kiln. The cautious potters want steady low heat, while younger artisans want a spectacular high-temperature furnace. The player completes both groups' duel trials and helps them build two linked chambers instead of choosing a permanent winner.

A cosmetic workshop can recolor deck boxes, card backs, and future player-room decorations. The duel rewards focus on Fire and Light utility cards already obtainable elsewhere, keeping the workshop desirable without making it compulsory.

**Anchor NPCs:**

- **Pella Ash, kiln keeper:** Defensive Fire/Light control and the conservative kiln trial.
- **Rusk, apprentice glazer:** Fast Fire Humans protected by cheap Light blockers.
- **Dovan Clay, token painter:** Sells cosmetics and uses a deliberately strange five-color casual deck.

## Second-tier towns

### Cloudrest Peak

**Side road:** Above Stormbreak Plateau  
**Visual identity:** Sheltered hot springs, white stone windbreaks, blue canvas roofs, and bells that warn of changing weather  
**Common decks:** Water/Fire tempo, shield triggers, bounce, and sudden counterattacks

Cloudrest began as a courier shelter and gradually became a small resort. Travelers come for the mineral baths, then stay because the locals treat every weather delay as an excuse to organize another duel bracket. It snows often at the peak.

The town's side story is deliberately lighthearted: its brass weather vane has vanished before the annual forecast contest. The culprit is a group of mountain birds decorating their nest, not a Hollow agent. The player follows scattered metal ribbons, duels the embarrassed contest entrants, and returns the vane after the nesting season ends.

Cloudrest provides free resting, a compact rematch gauntlet, and a rotating shop of Water and Fire shield triggers. It is a useful rest stop on the Tempest route, never a requirement for challenging Natasha.

**Anchor NPCs:**

- **Edda Springs, bathhouse keeper:** Water/Fire shield-trigger control.
- **Pax Bell, weather caller:** Tempo deck that times bounce before fast attacks.
- **Nim, junior courier:** A weaker Wave Striker deck that grows into a difficult rematch.

### Lanternfen

**Side road:** South of Gloam Quarry  
**Visual identity:** A misty wetland lit by cultivated glowing fungi, with plank paths and flat-bottomed pole boats  
**Common decks:** Darkness/Nature/Water graveyard setup, mana recovery, sacrifice, and recursion

Lanternfen is spooky only from a distance. Its residents are practical mushroom farmers who enjoy frightening visitors, then offering them soup. The settlement treats Darkness cards as part of ordinary life rather than evidence of tragedy or corruption.

The local story concerns wandering lights that have drawn tourists off the marked paths. Several children built remote lantern boats for a nighttime race and lost control of them in the reeds. The player recovers the boats through exploration and duels the adult night watch, who would prefer to blame ghosts rather than admit they were fooled.

The apothecary sells graveyard- and mana-oriented support cards. A repeatable board-state challenge asks the player to win after beginning with several cards in the graveyard, but its prizes remain gold and optional cards.

**Anchor NPCs:**

- **Auntie Morra, mushroom farmer:** Nature/Darkness recursion and the town's best deadpan dialogue.
- **Keel, night watch captain:** Darkness/Water control that saves removal for meaningful threats.
- **The Lantern Club:** Three children with simple mono-civilization decks and one coordinated rematch deck.

### Ribbonfair

**Side road:** Ribbonway Crossroads, shared with Dragon Keep and Mirror Arena<br>
**Visual identity:** A semi-permanent carnival of striped tents, painted wagons, paper streamers, and portable duel stages  
**Common decks:** Rotating civilizations and unusual theme restrictions rather than one regional archetype

Ribbonfair is a traveling market that has remained in the same meadow for so many seasons that everyone politely ignores the contradiction. Performers, card traders, retired competitors, and collectors use it as neutral ground between major Circuit venues.

Its side story follows auditions for a new headline duel show. The player can support a comedian, an illusionist, or a serious technical duelist through separate brackets. All three eventually become one ensemble, with the player's first choice determining the costumes and dialogue rather than a permanent gameplay advantage.

Ribbonfair offers rotating rule challenges such as creature-only decks, a shared civilization requirement, or a maximum card cost. Its prize counter focuses on cosmetics, alternate card backs, and cards available through other sources. This makes it a replayable post-game destination without turning it into required preparation for Sable or Caelum.

**Anchor NPCs:**

- **Vivi Ribbons, ringmaster:** Changes deck theme between rematches.
- **Malko Mirth, duel comedian:** Fire/Darkness tricks, sacrifice effects, and intentionally terrible jokes.
- **Seren Glass, illusionist:** Water/Light tapping and bounce with a carefully staged defensive finish.

## Relationship to the main campaign

Optional towns appear on the world map as short offshoot roads. They do not lie between the player and a required destination. Their map markers use a town symbol rather than the Circuit crest or Hollow-fragment symbols.

NPCs in these settlements know that the Circuit and Hollow crisis exist, but they are not secret Crest Holders, retired Collectors, missing researchers, or required witnesses. Their value comes from showing what ordinary people do with cards when the fate of the world is not immediately at stake.
