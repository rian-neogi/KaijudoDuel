# Glasswater Port

## Layout purpose

Glasswater should feel like a major port city rather than a compact regional town. It has several neighborhoods, a working commercial harbor, civic offices, residential canals, specialized markets, a large sanctioned arena, and old infrastructure beneath the streets.

The city is a required first-tier region containing the Tidal Crest and Water Resonance Seal. Its Watershed Gate enters the shared crossroads for Emberglen and Rootmaze Commons, while its western ferry route reaches the optional village of Reedwake.

## Navigation principles

- **Tideglass Square** is the central landmark and primary arrival point. The card shop, inn, city map, and routes to every ward are nearby.
- Four major wards use colored bridge rails and painted wave symbols: navy for the Grand Harbor, violet for the Ledger Ward, silver for Arena Isle, and green for the South Canals.
- The city should be visibly large enough that the player cannot see one edge from another. Long sightlines across water communicate scale without filling every block with collision.
- Main bridges remain fixed and at least three tiles wide. Smaller footbridges and ferries provide shortcuts but never become the only route to an essential service.
- Canals divide districts without making navigation tedious. Every important island or quay has at least two connections.
- Large buildings use distinct roof colors, flags, cranes, clocks, or towers so the player can navigate by silhouette.
- Cargo workers and patrol NPCs should move along broad quays rather than narrow bridges where they could obstruct the player.

## Central waterfront

### Tideglass Square

Tideglass Square is a broad stone plaza built over the meeting point of three canals. Pale translucent paving reveals water flowing beneath reinforced glass channels. At sunset the square glows blue and gold, making it the city's most recognizable location.

The square contains:

- A large tide clock showing harbor depth and ferry departures
- A public city map with ward colors and bridge closures
- A dry circular duel platform for casual matches
- Benches surrounding a fountain fed by the underground river
- A contract board for deliveries, trainer challenges, and missing cargo
- Covered arcades where temporary merchants set up stalls
- Direction markers for the harbor, Ledger Ward, Arena Isle, and southern roads

The player should first enter Glasswater here from the eastern road. Neris's representative explains the city and directs the player toward either the arena trial or the local Hollow investigation without forcing one immediate route.

### Current and Quill Card Exchange

Glasswater's primary card shop occupies a three-story blue building on the west side of the square. The ground floor sells cards, the second floor contains deck consultation tables, and the top floor stores registered trade inventory behind waterproof shutters.

The Exchange specializes in:

- Water draw, filtering, blockers, and bounce
- Darkness discard and targeted removal
- Liquid People evolution lines
- Shield-trigger creatures used by sailors
- Nature support imported from Rootmaze
- Rotating cards brought through Reedwake and Emberglen

The shop may offer a limited card-exchange service for excess commons. Mercer remains the world's broadest general merchant, while Current and Quill provides deeper regional Water/Darkness stock.

### The Gull and Lantern Inn

The city's largest inn faces the square with a painted gull holding a violet lantern above its door. It serves sailors, Circuit challengers, investigators, and merchants waiting for cargo inspections.

The ground floor includes a public dining hall, rumor board, and small stage. Upper floors contain ordinary rooms and a quieter wing reserved for registered challengers. During the main story, Marin and Tidal use a meeting room here when they are not at the prediction engine.

The inn provides:

- Rest and save services
- A future fast-travel point
- Visiting-NPC dialogue
- Regional side-quest notices
- A visible place for rival duelists to gather after their matches

### Glasswater General Market

Arcades south of the square form a permanent general market. Unlike the noisy Fish Market, this area sells finished goods and imported supplies.

Permanent businesses include:

- A waterproof clothing shop
- A map and writing-supply store
- A card-sleeve printer using translucent blue film
- A Rootmaze herb and tea counter
- A parcel office forwarding purchases toward Emberglen
- A small food hall serving dockworkers between shifts

## Grand Harbor Ward

### Long Quay

Long Quay is the commercial spine of Glasswater. Deep-water ships unload beside stone piers while smaller canal boats carry goods toward warehouses and inland towns. Cranes, cargo nets, bells, and painted lane markings make it visually busy without requiring every background worker to be interactive.

The quay contains several broad patrol routes suitable for moving trainer NPCs. Pell works here, usually beside stacked rope coils away from critical doors.

### Harbor Master's Office

The Harbor Master's Office is a squat teal-roofed building overlooking Long Quay. A signal balcony displays weather flags and docking assignments.

Inside are:

- The harbor schedule board
- A public counter for ferry and cargo questions
- A private chart room
- Records of damaged or delayed shipments
- An emergency bell connected to the Tideguard

Early investigation scenes can begin here when shipping predictions contradict the actual current.

### Tideguard House and Pier

The Tideguard patrols waterways, escorts valuable cargo, and rescues ships during sudden current changes. Its headquarters combines a watch house, equipment shed, and rapid-launch pier.

The building includes:

- A briefing room with route maps
- Rescue equipment and spare flotation gear
- A holding room for dangerous cargo rather than ordinary prisoners
- An honor wall listing past rescue crews
- A quiet office Tidal once used during his service

Tidal's return should prompt different dialogue from veteran guards who remember him. The headquarters helps explain his authority during the prediction-engine dispute without moving his permanent home away from Emberglen.

### Glasswater Shipyard

The shipyard occupies two dry docks east of Long Quay. Wooden walkways surround ships under repair, and a large blue crane forms one of the city's skyline landmarks.

The yard provides:

- A shipwright shop selling ropes and travel supplies
- Delivery and repair side quests
- A Fire/Water worker duelist who uses mechanical support cards
- A shortcut between the Grand Harbor and eastern breakwater
- Environmental storytelling through hulls from Reedwake, distant ports, and patrol fleets

One dry dock may temporarily close during the false-prediction crisis after a ship arrives at the wrong tide.

### Customs Warehouses

Three large glass-roofed warehouses stand behind Long Quay:

- **Warehouse Blue:** Food, textiles, and ordinary trade goods
- **Warehouse Violet:** Registered cards and sealed valuables
- **Warehouse Green:** Rootmaze timber, herbs, and creature-care supplies

Elevated inspection walks let clerks view cargo without blocking workers below. The Violet warehouse connects directly to the Ledger Ward records system and becomes important when stolen match data is hidden inside legitimate card shipments.

At least one warehouse should have an enterable interior with rows of crates, overhead windows, and alternate exits suitable for a short investigation sequence.

### Fish Market

The Fish Market sits between Long Quay and the Reedwake ferry. It is loud, wet, crowded, and markedly less formal than Tideglass Square.

Besides seafood stalls, it contains:

- A cookshop serving harbor meals
- A bait and river-supply vendor
- A casual duel corner marked with old rope
- A bulletin board for ferry crews
- A rotating Reedwake trader

The market's awnings change color by catch type, helping it remain visually readable despite many stalls.

### Reedwake Ferry Quay

The western quay serves small ferries traveling through shallow reed channels. It has a covered waiting area, hand-operated cargo crane, and ticket booth rather than a major terminal.

The ferry to Reedwake remains optional. Its departure board and distinctive green pennant advertise the side town without making the player pass through it for any Glasswater objective.

### Breakwaters and lighthouse

Two long breakwaters protect the harbor basin. The western arm ends near the ferry route, while the eastern arm supports a tall lighthouse faced with pale canal glass.

The lighthouse interior may contain:

- A spiral stair
- Weather instruments
- A panoramic overlook
- A hidden collectible or optional duelist
- A signal-control puzzle during the city crisis

The breakwaters should feel like optional exploration paths, not required walks between city services.

## Ledger Ward

### Port Authority Hall

The Port Authority is Glasswater's civic center. Its long navy roof, public clock, and rows of waterproof record windows distinguish it from the commercial warehouses.

The ground floor handles ordinary permits and complaints. Upper floors contain investigation offices and meeting chambers. A public gallery allows residents to observe major hearings, reflecting the city's attempt to rebuild trust after its old contraband scandal.

Rooms include:

- Registration hall
- Cargo arbitration chamber
- Public records desk
- Investigator bullpen
- Consent and privacy office
- Council meeting room
- Secure stair to the prediction-engine oversight level

### Neris Quill's office and residence

Neris occupies a corner office above the authority archives. It contains harbor maps, handwritten duel observations, several chairs positioned for interviews, and a wall of questions he has not yet answered.

A narrow internal stair reaches a modest apartment above the office. Neris keeps few trophies there; most shelves contain notebooks, tide charts, and returned objects from completed investigations.

The office functions as:

- Neris's primary dialogue location before the arena trial
- A place to review partial information about simulated opponents
- The investigation board for the stolen-memory plot
- A rematch registration point after earning the Tidal Crest

### Prediction Hall

Prediction Hall was designed as a public research building, not a hidden villain laboratory. Its architecture should initially appear useful and trustworthy: clean water channels, rotating model ships, probability boards, and observation galleries.

The building is divided into:

- A public demonstration chamber
- Three duel-simulation rooms
- A model harbor driven by controlled currents
- A voluntary match-record intake desk
- A sealed processing room added by later administrators
- An oversight gallery linking to Port Authority Hall

During the Hollow investigation, subtle differences reveal the abuse: unsigned records, sealed cabinets without public numbers, and outputs describing private decisions the system should not know.

After resolution, the processing room is dismantled or rebuilt under explicit consent rules. The useful tide and rescue models remain operational.

### Consent Archive

A smaller violet building behind Prediction Hall stores permission forms, deletion requests, and the original scope of donated match records. It becomes critical evidence that the engine exceeded what participants authorized.

The archive should reward careful exploration rather than a single glowing clue. The player compares timestamps, missing signatures, and transfer seals across several shelves with help from Marin or Tidal.

### Iri's Ledger Brokerage

Iri works from a narrow shop lined with account books and waterproof lockboxes. She verifies trades, estimates cargo risk, and arranges card transactions between crews.

Her exterior duel spot occupies a small courtyard beside the shop. After four victories, she can offer rotating economic dialogue and a discount on one regional service without becoming essential to story progression.

### Sol's Chart House

Sol's workshop has a wide sloped table visible through its front window. Rolled maps hang from ceiling racks, while colored compass cases identify road, canal, and lake charts.

The shop sells regional maps and provides hints about unopened roads. During the crisis, Sol overlays actual journeys with prediction-engine routes to demonstrate that the system is concealing rather than merely misreading information.

### Clerk Row

Several attached homes house port clerks, analysts, teachers, and their families. Narrow balconies overlook quieter canals, and shared courtyards provide laundry lines, small gardens, and children's duel tables.

At least two residences should be enterable:

- A clerk family's apartment containing ordinary civic life and concern over stolen records
- A retired investigator's home containing history from the contraband scandal

Other homes can use varied shutters, balcony plants, and evening window lights to make the city feel populated.

## Arena Isle

### Tidal Arena

Glasswater's sanctioned arena occupies a broad artificial island reached by two permanent bridges and one public ferry. Its layered blue roof resembles overlapping waves, while a violet stripe acknowledges the Darkness cards common in Neris's deck.

The complex contains:

- Main duel floor with tiered seating
- Registration and deck-check hall
- Challenger preparation rooms
- Neris's analysis booth
- Public replay boards using consent-approved match summaries
- A covered practice court
- A waterside terrace for post-match dialogue

The official Tidal Crest match begins only after the player completes the information trial. The island remains accessible beforehand for practice duels and spectators.

### Simulation Rooms

Three smaller duel chambers connect the arena to Prediction Hall's public systems. Each presents partial information about a simulated opponent and asks the player to prepare accordingly.

The rooms should differ visually:

- **Current Room:** Water tempo and changing board positions
- **Anchor Room:** Light or Water defense and patient resource play
- **Undertow Room:** Darkness discard and concealed threats

The player may tackle them in any order and return to the city between matches.

### Challenger Lodge

The lodge houses sanctioned competitors separately from the central inn. It includes small rooms, deck-building tables, lockers, showers, and a quiet courtyard.

Marin may stay here while researching the engine, while Tidal refuses the formal room and uses the Gull and Lantern with ordinary travelers. Their choices reinforce their personalities without changing services.

### Practice Docks

Low platforms behind the arena provide room for casual matches and trainer NPCs. Pell may move here after his first defeat, allowing rematches away from the busiest cargo routes.

Practice Docks also host youth leagues and post-Crest exhibition duels, making Arena Isle useful after the main match.

## South Canal residential district

### Canal Homes

The southern district contains most of Glasswater's permanent population. Rows of two- and three-story homes face narrow canals, with small footbridges connecting shared courtyards.

Residential variations include:

- Dockworker homes with gear racks and early shift lanterns
- Merchant houses with ground-floor storerooms
- Canal-keeper cottages beside lock controls
- Older stone homes built before the modern harbor
- Newer apartments for arena staff and students

Not every house needs an interior, but streets should show residents cooking, repairing nets, carrying records, tending balcony plants, and playing informal duels.

### Glassgarden Park

Glassgarden is a public park built around shallow geometric pools. Transparent stepping stones make fish and water plants visible below. Trees imported from Rootmaze provide shade, linking the city's engineered water culture with Nature.

The park includes:

- A children's duel circle
- Quiet benches for NPC conversations
- A memorial to Tideguard rescue crews
- A small tea pavilion
- An entrance to the Deepwell House

During the crisis, contradictory prediction notices cause residents to avoid the park despite calm water, making it feel unnaturally empty.

### South Canal School

The school teaches ordinary subjects alongside navigation, trade arithmetic, and consent rules for recording duels. Its yard contains a painted practice board and miniature lock system.

A teacher can offer tutorial challenges about hand size, card draw, or deciding when bounce is useful. The school closes only briefly during the worst story state and never hides an essential service.

### Public kitchens and washhouse

A long communal building combines laundry platforms, heated baths, and inexpensive kitchens. Water flows constantly through filtration beds before rejoining the canals.

This is a strong location for everyday NPC dialogue and visual activity. It also provides an alternate rest service for players exploring the southern district.

## Deepwell infrastructure and Water Sanctuary

### Deepwell House

Deepwell House is a circular civic pump station at the edge of Glassgarden. Its lower machinery regulates the clear underground river that feeds every canal.

The public upper floor contains:

- Water-level gauges
- An educational model of the city canals
- Maintenance schedules
- A viewing window over the main lifting wheel
- Stairs to restricted cistern levels

### Underglass Cisterns

Beneath the city lies a network of old translucent-stone reservoirs. These chambers should feel ancient and quiet in contrast to the busy streets above. Lit water behind pale walls creates moving blue patterns without making the route visually confusing.

The cistern layout includes:

- Main pump chamber
- Overflow galleries
- Inspection walkways
- An abandoned pre-city channel
- Three control locks corresponding to major surface canals
- The sanctuary approach

During the crisis, false prediction outputs redirect water through unsafe channels. The player restores the locks using evidence gathered above rather than guessing a single correct lever sequence.

### Water Resonance Sanctuary

The Water Seal rests where the underground river first splits toward the harbor and residential canals. Early engineers built around it rather than moving it, treating the stable current as the reason Glasswater could exist.

The sanctuary is a round chamber containing a central pool, five inlet arches, and a narrow observation ring. Water reflections show authentic remembered routes; the stolen-memory corruption introduces movements that have no matching source.

Restoring the Seal requires resolving the consent conflict in Prediction Hall and correcting the cistern flow. The Tidal Crest duel is related thematically but is not a magical requirement for entering the sanctuary.

## Regional exits

### Watershed Gate to Emberglen and Rootmaze Commons

The city's sole inland portal leaves through a broad customs arch southeast of the South Canals and enters Watershed Crossroads. The shared route divides outside the city toward Emberglen and Rootmaze Commons. Blue-and-gold markers identify the Emberglen branch, while blue-and-green railings identify the managed stream toward Rootmaze.

Mercer's cargo wagons and Rootmaze handcarts use the same gate, so it remains wide, obvious, and permanently bidirectional after Act I. A parcel depot beside the arch sorts goods for both destinations and provides dialogue when Mercer's inventory expands. This three-way connector preserves the first-tier loop without maintaining two separate Glasswater road maps.

### West Ferry to Reedwake

The optional Reedwake connection leaves from the ferry quay. Once introduced, ferries run on a visible schedule but should not force the player to wait in real time. Speaking to the ferrymaster triggers the transition.

## Suggested NPC placement

- **Neris Quill:** Port Authority office initially; Prediction Hall, Arena Isle, and waterside terrace as the story advances
- **Pell:** Long Quay or Fish Market; Practice Docks for later rematches
- **Iri:** Ledger Brokerage courtyard
- **Sol:** Chart House, city map, or a canal overlook during investigations
- **Marin:** Prediction Hall, Consent Archive, or Challenger Lodge
- **Tidal:** Tideguard House, Gull and Lantern meeting room, or Deepwell approach
- **Harbor Master:** Office balcony and Long Quay
- **Card merchant:** Current and Quill Exchange
- **Innkeeper:** Gull and Lantern front desk or dining hall
- **Minor residents:** Markets, Clerk Row courtyards, Canal Homes, park, shipyard, and ferry queue

Challenge NPCs should patrol plazas, quays, and practice courts rather than bridges, authority doors, or narrow residential walks.

## Story-state changes

### Before the prediction crisis

- Harbor traffic follows normal schedules
- Prediction Hall demonstrations attract visitors
- All three simulation rooms operate publicly
- Deepwell gauges agree with the tide clock
- Arena crowds and markets make the city feel busy

### During the stolen-memory investigation

- Prediction notices contradict actual ship arrivals
- One dry dock contains a vessel damaged by a false tide instruction
- Unsigned record crates appear in Warehouse Violet
- Residents argue near Port Authority Hall
- Some public replay boards display information they should not possess
- Cistern locks divert water from one optional shortcut

### After restoring the Water Seal

- Harbor schedules and actual currents agree again
- The unauthorized processing room is closed or rebuilt transparently
- Reedwake trade and Emberglen parcel traffic increase
- Current and Quill expands its regional stock
- Neris's Tidal Crest match and advanced rematches remain available
- A fast canal ferry opens between Tideglass Square and Arena Isle

### During Act III

- Blank reflections appear briefly in canal surfaces
- Clerks replace failing enchanted signs with handwritten schedules
- Tideguard patrols protect evacuation and supply routes
- The shop, inn, all regional exits, and essential bridges remain usable
- Restoring optional city blocks returns crowds, banners, and recorded match summaries

## Implementation notes

- Glasswater should use a large variable-size map with camera scrolling rather than compressing every ward into one viewport.
- Keep Tideglass Square, the card shop, inn, and city map close to the first-arrival point.
- Use canals and skyline landmarks to communicate scale, but provide frequent bridges and clear loops.
- The Grand Harbor can use animated background ships and cranes without making every vessel an interactable object.
- Separate dense pedestrian areas from NPC challenge patrols to prevent accidental movement blocking.
- Interior maps are appropriate for Port Authority Hall, Prediction Hall, the arena, at least one warehouse, the inn, and the Underglass Cisterns.
- Maintain a surface route between all essential buildings even when story-state shortcuts close.
- Outdoor tiles should combine water, paths, marble or pale paving, wooden docks, buildings, counters, and workshop props consistently with the conventions in `AGENTS.md`.
