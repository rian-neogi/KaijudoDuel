# Stonechant Village

## Role and identity

Stonechant is an optional first-tier village reached from Rootmaze Commons. It
contains no Circuit Crest, Resonance Seal, or mandatory Hollow clue. Its purpose
is to give Mystery Totems a cultural home, provide a concentrated Totem card
pool, and show a form of Nature practice that is based on attention and
reciprocity rather than simple wilderness strength.

The village should feel old without feeling frozen in time. Homes circle a
field of resonant standing stones, workshops carve replacement markers and
duel tables, and living Totem creatures wander through public spaces as
neighbors rather than ceremonial props.

**Planned region ID:** `stonechant`

**Common decks:** Nature-based Mystery Totem decks, with Fire Dragons, Light
guardians, Water utility, or Darkness graveyard tools used to support different
Totem abilities.

## Background

Stonechant began where Rootmaze pathkeepers discovered a ring of stones that
hummed when nearby creatures changed the land. The sound was not speech, but it
carried stable patterns: warning before floods, a low pulse when nesting grounds
were disturbed, and complex harmonies during friendly duels.

Early settlers learned that Mystery Totems responded to those patterns. They
did not create or command the creatures. Instead, they built a village where
people could compare what they heard and test interpretations in public. The
title **shaman** came to mean a trained listener who can explain a resonance
pattern, admit uncertainty, and accept correction from another listener.

This distinction matters to the village. A carved image is only a sign showing
where a Totem has chosen to gather; it is never treated as the creature itself.
Old carvings are repaired, repurposed, or returned to the earth when their
associated Totem moves on.

Oren Canopy studied in Stonechant before becoming Rootmaze's Crest Holder.
Cryptic Totem taught Oren that a powerful pattern can protect a community while
still leaving room for disagreement. Stonechant residents are proud of Oren,
but refuse to claim ownership of either the duelist or the lesson.

## Layout

```text
                         Echo Grove
                             |
                  North Listening Terrace
                             |
       Carvers' Row ---- Chorus Green ---- Guest Hearth
             |               |                  |
       Totem Nursery ---- Rootwell ---- Trading Circle
                             |
                    South Trail Gate
                             |
                  Whisperroot Trail to Rootmaze
```

The main path runs north from the Rootmaze gate to Chorus Green and the Echo
Grove. Two compact side loops connect the workshops, nursery, guest hearth, and
market, so the village is easy to navigate without becoming a single corridor.

### South Trail Gate

The entrance uses green Rootmaze markers beside gray spiral stones. A covered
notice board explains village etiquette: do not block a Totem's path, do not
touch active listening stones, and do not call every unusual noise a prophecy.

### Chorus Green

The central common is an oval of packed earth surrounded by resonant stones of
different sizes. Ordinary conversations, lessons, celebrations, and public
duels all happen here. During a match, the stones answer card echoes with soft
rhythms that change according to mana use, tapping, and creature movement.

The green contains three duel tables rather than a formal arena. Stonechant has
no Crest Holder and does not pretend that local custom needs Circuit prestige
to be meaningful.

### Echo Grove

The northern grove is home to several Mystery Totems. Paths curve around their
preferred clearings instead of cutting through them. Complete Totem sprites and
large trees should create strong vertical layers, with canopy foreground tiles
obscuring portions of the path without hiding its direction.

The grove supports a short optional quest in which competing rhythms cause two
Totem groups to avoid the same spring. The solution is to observe both groups,
then arrange a duel whose turns reproduce a compatible shared rhythm. The
conflict is resolved through listening, not defeating a wild creature.

### North Listening Terrace

Apprentice shamans practice distinguishing weather, creature movement, card
echoes, and wishful thinking. The terrace contains movable sounding boards and
water bowls that visualize vibration. NPC dialogue changes after the local
quest as apprentices revise their original theories.

### Carvers' Row

Open-front workshops produce signposts, duel counters, musical slats, and
protective cases for cards. The work uses fallen wood and retired marker stone;
cutting a living Totem tree or active listening stone is forbidden.

### Totem Nursery

The nursery is a recovery ground for young or injured Mystery Totems. It is not
a pet shop. Players can help gather food, repair a rain shelter, and duel a
caretaker using a low-cost Totem deck designed to demonstrate race synergy.

### Guest Hearth

A longhouse provides beds, food, deck tables, and stories from traveling
listeners. Its walls display conflicting maps of the same old journeys to
reinforce that interpretation is a community process rather than revelation by
one authority.

### Trading Circle

Stonechant's specialist card stall sells Mystery Totems and the support cards
needed to build around them. Early stock should include accessible cards such
as Jiggly Totem, Whispering Totem, Shaman Broccoli, and Spinning Totem. Later
stock can add Clobber Totem, Carnival Totem, Bliss Totem, Forbidding Totem,
Vreemah, Dracodance Totem, Rollicking Totem, and Cursed Totem.

The strongest Totems should unlock through local duels, quests, or late stock
rather than all appearing on the first visit.

## Duel culture

Stonechant duelists announce what pattern they are testing before a match. One
may build around tapped abilities, another around graveyard-to-mana recovery,
and another around Dragons that cooperate with Dracodance or Rollicking Totem.
Mixed civilizations are common because the supporting relationship matters
more than matching card colors for its own sake.

Suggested local opponents include:

- A young listener using repeated Whispering Totems and mana growth
- A caretaker using Shaman Broccoli, Jiggly Totem, and creature recovery
- A traveling engineer using Techno Totem with Light tapping effects
- A dragon caller using Dracodance Totem and Rollicking Totem
- The senior chorus keeper using a late-game Mystery Totem toolbox

Rewards should collectively let the player assemble a functional Totem deck
without requiring every rare card. The senior keeper's final reward can be
Forbidding Totem or Vreemah, Freaky Mojo Totem.

## Anchor NPCs

- **Eira Bellroot, senior chorus keeper:** An adaptive Totem toolbox led by
  Forbidding Totem. Eira teaches that authority means making an interpretation
  testable, not making it unquestionable.
- **Tovin Knock, apprentice listener:** Repeated Whispering Totems and simple
  mana growth. His enthusiasm produces several confidently wrong readings.
- **Sumi Coil, sounding-board engineer:** Techno Totem with Light tapping and
  defensive support. She maintains the terrace instruments.
- **Kes Emberstep, dragon caller:** Dracodance Totem and Rollicking Totem with a
  compact Dragon package. Kes treats every duel as percussion rehearsal.
- **Mallow, nursery keeper:** Shaman Broccoli, Jiggly Totem, and recovery tools.
  Mallow trades common Totem cards after the nursery quest.

## Hollow-story response

Stonechant is not a hidden sixth sanctuary. During the Fading, its stones repeat
perfect but lifeless rhythms copied from earlier gatherings. The residents can
detect that the patterns are counterfeit because nobody disagrees with them and
no creature alters them.

This becomes an optional thematic clue: a memory can be accurate in detail and
still be false as a living relationship. Completing the village quest provides
an ally or card reward for Act III, but the Archive gate never requires it.

## Visual direction

- Warm gray standing stones with subtle civilization-colored resonance bands
- Mossy timber homes with steep leaf roofs and open workshop fronts
- Hanging wooden slats, water bowls, spiral path markers, and low fire circles
- Large trees around the perimeter and smaller complete Totem clearings inside
- No generic skull masks, costume feathers, or imagery borrowed from a real
  culture; Stonechant's visual language should arise from this world's cards

## Implementation notes

- Add Stonechant as a planned `town` region only when its exterior is painted.
- Keep the village optional and permanently bidirectional after Rootmaze opens.
- Use `Whisperroot Trail` as its connector region.
- Give every local duelist a distinct Totem interaction rather than five copies
  of the same Nature-ramp deck.
- Place Totem creatures as complete object or character sprites, never partial
  tiles cut from forest-fill artwork.
