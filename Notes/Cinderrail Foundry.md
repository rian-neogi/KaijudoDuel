# Cinderrail Foundry

## Layout purpose

Cinderrail should feel like a working industrial town whose foundry, railway, arena, and homes grew together. It is loud and energetic, but not polluted misery: heat is controlled, safety is a public value, and residents take pride in maintaining machinery that keeps the wider region connected.

The town is a required first-tier region containing the Forge Crest and Fire Resonance Seal. It connects directly to Emberglen, Rootmaze Commons, Gloam Quarry, and the optional pottery town of Clayhearth.

## Navigation principles

- **Forge Square** is the central pedestrian landmark. The card shop, inn, assembly hall, station approach, and foundry entrance are visible from it.
- Rail tracks and heat channels communicate the town's function but should not fragment navigation. Every required crossing has a broad bridge, marked underpass, or guarded platform.
- Major routes use painted metal strips: gold toward Emberglen, green toward Rootmaze, violet toward Gloam, and white toward Clayhearth.
- Red-brick industrial buildings must have distinct silhouettes: sawtooth foundry roofs, a tall station clock, three furnace stacks, and the arena's shield-shaped canopy.
- Machinery may animate in the background, but essential routes must not depend on waiting for trains, cranes, or timed hazards.
- Worker NPCs should patrol plazas, platforms, and wide catwalks rather than narrow rail crossings.
- Heat effects must preserve card, sign, and character readability. Use localized shimmer and orange light instead of covering the screen with smoke.

## Central civic district

### Forge Square

Forge Square is a broad paved yard between the station and cooperative foundry. A decommissioned rail turntable forms a circular public platform at its center. Workers use it for announcements, equipment demonstrations, casual duels, and town votes.

The square contains:

- A large shift clock synchronized with the rail timetable
- A public map showing roads, rail platforms, and furnace status
- A duel circle painted on the old turntable
- Covered benches made from retired carriage frames
- Water barrels and clearly marked emergency equipment
- A contract board for deliveries, repairs, trainer challenges, and apprenticeships
- Four route signs pointing toward the regional exits

The player should arrive near the square on their first visit. Brassa addresses the town from the turntable during the opening foundry scene.

### Workers' Assembly Hall

The Assembly Hall is the town's civic center and union house. It has a red-brick ground floor, pale reinforced upper walls, and a wide doorway designed for crowds leaving a shift together.

Inside are:

- A tiered public meeting chamber
- The cooperative ownership ledger
- Safety reports and repair schedules
- A voting board with removable name plates
- A small arbitration room
- Storage for festival banners and duel-club equipment
- A rear office used for crisis planning

The Curator's machinery dispute is debated here. Brassa initially argues that an immediate shutdown would strand neighboring towns, while Flint presents evidence that the rail power and resonance harvester can be separated.

### Spark and Bastion Cardworks

The regional card shop combines a retail counter with a small print-and-repair workshop. Its sign shows a flame protected by a white shield, reflecting Cinderrail's Fire/Light identity.

The shop specializes in:

- Low-cost Fire Humans and Dragonoids
- Light blockers that protect evolution material
- Tapping and anti-blocker support
- Armored Blaster evolution lines
- Dragon cost support imported through the rail network
- Fire and Light shield triggers

The workshop can add industrial card sleeves, metal-edged deck boxes, or Forge-themed cosmetics. Mercer remains the general collection hub; Cardworks offers deeper local archetype support rather than irreplaceable staples.

### The Rivet and Rest Inn

The inn occupies a converted rail hotel beside Forge Square. Its long common room serves meals throughout the shift cycle, so the building never fully closes.

The inn provides:

- Rest and save services
- A future fast-travel marker
- Rooms for Circuit challengers and visiting specialists
- A public deck-building table
- A notice board for regional side quests
- A quieter meeting room used by Flint and Mercer during Act II

The interior displays route maps, old rail tickets, and repaired tools donated by former guests.

### Shift Market

The market becomes busiest when whistles announce a crew change. Stalls sit beneath the elevated station approach and are protected from sparks by pale Light-treated awnings.

Permanent stalls include:

- Prepared food for workers beginning or ending shifts
- Rootmaze lumber and herb supplies
- Emberglen produce and festival goods
- Protective clothing and goggles
- Clayhearth cookware and painted duel tokens
- A rotating traveling-card counter

Crowd density and stall inventory can change by time or story state without blocking the station route.

## Cinderrail Station and rail district

### Central Station

Central Station is the town's tallest civic building after the furnace stacks. Its clock tower, arched iron-and-glass roof, and five parallel platforms make it visible from most outdoor districts.

The main concourse contains:

- Ticket and freight counters
- A large regional route board
- Waiting benches and a food kiosk
- Parcel lockers
- A lost-property office
- Stairs to an elevated viewing bridge
- An exit directly onto Forge Square

Passenger rail may not yet be a player transport system, but trains and freight activity should make the town feel connected to a larger economy.

### Signal Tower

The Signal Tower overlooks the northern rail throat and ridge tunnel. Operators use mechanical arms, colored lamps, and resonance gauges to keep freight separated safely.

Ansa frequently visits the tower for route assignments. During the foundry crisis, false load reports cause signals to contradict the physical tracks, creating an investigation scene that parallels—but does not duplicate—Glasswater's prediction problem.

### Ridge Tunnel

The original tunnel through the ridge marks Cinderrail's founding achievement. Its stone portal bears the names of the Fire cutters and Light wardens who built it together.

The tunnel is mostly background infrastructure. A guarded maintenance entrance can support an optional item route or late-game shortcut without making players walk along active tracks.

### Rail Yard

The Rail Yard contains switching tracks, freight platforms, cranes, water towers, and repair sheds. Broad fenced walkways let the player cross safely while trains move in background lanes.

Key locations include:

- Freight classification platform
- Rootmaze goods shed
- Emberglen parcel depot
- Empty carriage storage
- Heavy crane yard
- Locomotive maintenance shed
- A safe practice space used by rail workers after shifts

Kipp may work near the maintenance shed before moving to the apprentice district for rematches.

### Courier Depot

The eastern depot coordinates road couriers when rail lines do not reach a destination. Ansa keeps schedules, maps, spare tack, and weather gear here.

The depot supports:

- Delivery side quests
- Regional route updates
- Ansa's duel and rematches
- Visible card shipments bound for Mercer
- A future fast-travel or parcel-delivery system

Its yard should be large enough for moving courier NPCs without interfering with the rail platform.

## Cooperative Foundry district

### Foundry Hall

Foundry Hall is a huge sawtooth-roofed structure containing controlled casting lines, assembly bays, and repair stations. High windows and roof vents keep the interior bright enough to navigate.

The public route through the hall includes:

- Visitor safety checkpoint
- Observation balcony
- Small casting demonstration line
- Tool storage
- Quality-inspection tables
- Assembly floor
- Enclosed passage toward the resonance furnaces

Background workers can animate at safe distances. The player should never need to cross an active pour lane or dodge machinery.

### Brassa's workshop and loft

Brassa maintains a practical workshop overlooking the assembly floor. The lower room contains tools, rail models, damaged arena components, and a large testing bench. A metal stair leads to a sparse residential loft.

The workshop functions as:

- Brassa's main dialogue location between furnace trials
- The investigation board for the hidden resonance machinery
- A place where Flint identifies familiar components
- Forge Crest rematch registration after the official duel
- A visual record of repairs completed across the campaign

Brassa's Forge Crest gauntlet is stored in a locked wall case only when she is not wearing it.

### Pattern Library

The Pattern Library stores molds, engineering drawings, tested procedures, and failure reports. Cinderrail considers documented mistakes valuable public property rather than embarrassing secrets.

Rooms include:

- Open drawing tables
- Fireproof plan cabinets
- Material sample shelves
- A copying desk
- A sealed archive for dangerous machinery

The Curator's collectors exploit an old unchecked equipment pattern. Comparing revisions here reveals where their additions entered the system.

### Scrapyard and reclamation shop

The fenced reclamation yard sorts metal by whether it can be repaired, recast, or safely discarded. It should look organized rather than like random debris.

The yard provides:

- Salvage gathering quests
- Flint's dismantling scenes
- A Fire/Darkness duelist using destruction-themed cards
- Cosmetic scrap-metal card backs
- An optional shortcut between Foundry Hall and the furnace district

One sealed crate bears Mercer's former caravan mark, establishing his accidental connection to the archive equipment without implying wrongdoing.

## The three resonance furnaces

The three furnaces form a visible triangular complex southeast of Foundry Hall. Each has a different role, stack shape, and trial. Elevated protected walkways connect them to a central control platform.

### Furnace One — Ignition

Ignition is the oldest active furnace and supplies rapid high heat for starting new production runs. Its stack is short and wide, surrounded by red warning lamps.

Its trial emphasizes acting quickly with limited resources. The associated duelist uses fast Fire attackers but punishes reckless shield attacks. Activating Ignition first produces immediate power while increasing strain elsewhere.

### Furnace Two — Bastion

Bastion uses Light resonance shields to contain heat and protect workers. Its pale segmented chimney and shield-shaped control house make it easy to identify.

Its trial emphasizes blockers, tapping, and protecting valuable creatures. Activating Bastion first makes the later furnace sequence safer but slower.

Holt guards this furnace and explains why containment is an active skill rather than passive fear.

### Furnace Three — Drive

Drive converts stable furnace output into rail and arena power. Its tall narrow stack stands beside flywheels and insulated power channels.

Its trial combines Fire pressure with Nature-style resource development or mechanical setup. Activating Drive too early creates the optional overload encounter established in Brassa's trial.

### Central control platform

The control platform displays each furnace's heat, shield stability, rail load, and resonance output. The player chooses activation order here after defeating the assigned duelists.

The interface should clearly communicate consequences without implying one required answer. Different safe orders can alter dialogue, environmental shortcuts, or the optional encounter while still allowing story completion.

### Hidden harvesting manifold

The Curator's collectors installed a concealed pipe-and-crystal network beneath the legitimate controls. It siphons resonance from public duels and disguises the load as ordinary arena consumption.

The manifold becomes visible in stages:

- Flint recognizes nonstandard fasteners
- A Pattern Library drawing lacks the added pipes
- Furnace readings disagree with rail output
- Mercer's old caravan mark identifies how the sealed parts arrived
- A maintenance crawlspace reaches the independent cutoff

Separating the manifold allows the town to keep essential power running while ending the forced-duel harvest.

## First Kiln and Fire Sanctuary

### The First Kiln

Beneath the modern furnace complex is the original stone kiln used by Cinderrail's founders. It is smaller, quieter, and heated by a stable natural vent rather than industrial machinery.

The town preserved it as a teaching space. Apprentices light their first controlled fires here and study old accounts of both successful and failed construction attempts.

### Fire Resonance Sanctuary

The Fire Seal rests in a chamber behind the First Kiln where the natural vent divides into several manageable channels. The sanctuary represents directed will: heat becomes useful when given purpose and containment.

The chamber contains:

- A central stone brazier
- Five controllable heat channels
- Founder name plates
- Light-treated safety screens
- An observation ring
- The original ridge-tunnel cutting tools

During the crisis, the hidden manifold pulls resonance upward from this chamber. Restoring the Seal requires isolating the harvester and stabilizing legitimate furnace loads. Winning the Forge Crest is thematically connected but not a magical requirement for sanctuary access.

## Forge Arena district

### Forge Arena

The sanctioned arena stands between Foundry Hall and the furnace complex. Its red canopy resembles overlapping armor plates, while pale structural ribs echo the blockers supporting Brassa's aggressive deck.

The arena includes:

- Main duel floor
- Worker-built tiered seating
- Registration and deck-check office
- Two challenger workshops instead of conventional dressing rooms
- Equipment inspection bay
- Brassa's captain platform
- Public display showing current furnace status
- A rear route toward the three furnace trials

The arena remains open for local league matches before and after the official Forge Crest duel.

### Workers' Duel Club

A lower hall beneath the seating contains the original after-shift club. It has mismatched tables, donated chairs, deck lockers, and a wall listing apprentices whose entry fees the club sponsored.

The club provides:

- Casual and repeatable matches
- Beginner deck advice
- Kipp's later rematches
- Tournament brackets
- Dialogue showing that Cinderrail's duel culture belongs to workers rather than only its Crest Holder

### Challenger workshops

Each challenger room includes a deck table, tool bench, water station, and board for planning attack sequences. The rooms reinforce the local belief that a deck should be prepared like reliable equipment.

## Worker residential district

### Crew Row

Rows of red-brick and pale-plaster homes stand south of Forge Square. Shared courtyards contain laundry lines, tool-cleaning sinks, small gardens, and shaded children's duel circles.

Housing variations include:

- Older foreman houses with ground-floor workshops
- Compact apprentice dormitories
- Family homes with rear vegetable plots
- Rail-worker apartments near the station
- Furnace-crew homes with extra washrooms and gear storage

At least three interiors should be accessible to show that Cinderrail is a community, not only a factory.

### Kipp's riveter house

Kipp rents a small workshop-home at the edge of Crew Row. Practice plates, rivet patterns, orange goggles, and half-built deck cases cover the workbench.

The yard provides enough open space for his first duel. Afterward he moves between his home, Apprentice Hall, and the Workers' Duel Club.

### Holt's guard quarters

Holt lives in a simple room attached to the furnace guard station. His equipment is meticulously arranged, while a shelf of brightly painted souvenirs undermines his severe appearance.

The quarters may become accessible after the player proves that one of Holt's safety reports was altered by the collectors.

### Cooperative Canteen

The canteen serves inexpensive meals around the clock. Long tables mix crews that otherwise work in separate districts, making it an ideal location for rumors, arguments, and post-trial dialogue.

The kitchen sources food from Emberglen and Honeyreach, while Clayhearth supplies most cookware. Menu boards change by shift and story state.

### Bathhouse and clinic

A combined washhouse and clinic stands beside a cool-water reservoir. Light specialists treat burns and eye strain, inspect safety gear, and enforce mandatory recovery periods.

The building provides:

- Rest or healing services
- Story dialogue about the human cost of unsafe furnace loads
- A visible safety culture
- A temporary treatment location after the optional overload encounter

Essential care remains available during every story state.

## Apprentice district

### Apprentice Hall

Apprentice Hall combines classrooms, practice workshops, and dormitory rooms. Its entrance displays two rules: “Ask before guessing” and “Test before scaling.”

Facilities include:

- Tool-safety classroom
- Small casting lab
- Deck-construction classroom
- Fireproof practice duel room
- Evolution-material lesson boards
- Student common room

Tutorial challenges can teach attack sequencing, preserving evolution bait, blocker removal, and deciding when aggression is sufficiently supported.

### Training yard

The open yard contains inert machinery, practice rails, and marked safety zones. Ansa sometimes runs courier-loading drills here, while Kipp tests apprentice duelists.

NPC challenges should occur inside the wide marked circles so spectators never block the through-route.

### Furnace Guard Station

The guard station controls access to the furnace bridges and First Kiln. Its pale reinforced walls, square tower, and large status lamps distinguish it from ordinary workshops.

The station includes:

- Safety briefing counter
- Equipment lockers
- Furnace access ledger
- Emergency control room
- Holt's office
- Stairway to the lower sanctuary route

The official furnace trial begins here after the player receives clearance from Brassa.

## Regional roads

### West Road to Emberglen

The primary road passes beneath the elevated rail approach and leaves west of Central Station. Gold route plates and frequent wagon pull-offs make it suitable for Mercer's shipments.

The route remains permanently bidirectional after Act I, allowing the player to return to Emberglen between any furnace or arena objective.

### Northwest greenway to Rootmaze Commons

The Rootmaze connection leaves beside Crew Row and transitions from brick paving to flexible timber bridges. Green markers and water barrels identify the shared maintenance route.

This road creates a loop between first-tier regions. Trade platforms transfer herbs, living timber, and creature-care supplies without forcing travel through Emberglen.

### South Road to Gloam Quarry

The Gloam road begins near the First Kiln and follows a reinforced stone descent. Violet markers appear beside old black-stone retaining walls.

Although physically present earlier, its outer checkpoint opens with the Confluence relay. Once unlocked, it remains a direct bidirectional connection between the regions.

### East artisan road to Clayhearth

The optional Clayhearth road branches from the freight yard and passes stacks of safely cooled ceramic material. White chimney symbols advertise the pottery town.

The route never lies between the player and a Cinderrail objective. Clayhearth kilns may be visible in the distance to encourage optional exploration.

## Suggested NPC placement

- **Brassa Vale:** Forge Square initially; workshop, control platform, and Forge Arena as the story progresses
- **Kipp:** Maintenance shed or home yard; Apprentice Hall and Duel Club for rematches
- **Ansa:** Courier Depot, Signal Tower, or Training Yard
- **Holt:** Bastion Furnace, Guard Station, or clinic after an overload
- **Flint:** Scrapyard, Pattern Library, or hidden-manifold access
- **Mercer:** Temporary investigation scenes in the inn meeting room; his shop remains in Emberglen
- **Card merchant:** Spark and Bastion Cardworks
- **Innkeeper:** Rivet and Rest common room
- **Minor residents:** Station concourse, Shift Market, Crew Row, canteen, rail yard walks, and arena seating

Trainer NPCs should use plazas, practice yards, and wide industrial walks rather than tracks, furnace bridges, or building entrances.

## Story-state changes

### Before discovering the harvester

- All three furnaces appear operational
- Rail traffic follows ordinary schedules
- Workers debate unusual energy losses but continue normal shifts
- The arena display attributes excess load to public matches
- The First Kiln remains accessible for supervised lessons

### During the furnace crisis

- One rail platform closes because of unstable power
- Harvester pipes become visible beneath furnace walkways
- Shift Market conversations divide over shutting down the works
- Flint occupies the Scrapyard investigation area
- Furnace trials and activation order alter lights, sound, and optional routes
- The clinic treats workers affected by false load readings

### After restoring the Fire Seal

- Legitimate furnace output stabilizes
- Rail platforms and regional freight reopen
- The hidden manifold is dismantled and displayed as evidence
- Cardworks and Shift Market gain new stock
- Brassa's Forge Crest duel and advanced rematches remain available
- The Rootmaze loop and Clayhearth trade become busier

### During Act III

- Some enchanted gauges lose their labels, so workers hang handwritten replacements
- Pale blank patches briefly appear in furnace flames
- The cooperative prioritizes supply routes to Emberglen and Crown Gate
- All essential shops, regional roads, and safe crossings remain usable
- Optional repair quests restore machinery animation, banners, and worker crowds

## Implementation notes

- Use a large scrolling map with Forge Square near the center and strong skyline landmarks in every direction.
- Animate trains, cranes, flywheels, and furnace light primarily in background layers to avoid timing-dependent traversal.
- Keep all active rail crossings fenced and visually safe; the player should never be struck by a train.
- Provide at least two surface routes between Forge Square, the station, worker district, foundry, and arena.
- Interior maps are appropriate for Foundry Hall, Brassa's workshop, Assembly Hall, Cardworks, the inn, Apprentice Hall, Forge Arena, and the First Kiln sanctuary.
- Furnace-state changes should swap controlled props, lights, sounds, and barriers rather than rewrite the whole town map.
- Use red brick, metal catwalks, pale safety barriers, path tiles, counters, workshop tools, and controlled fire assets consistently with `AGENTS.md`.
- Keep important walkways at least two tiles wide so wandering workers and duelists cannot trap the player.
