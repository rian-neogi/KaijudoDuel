# Gloam Quarry

## Layout purpose

Gloam Quarry should feel like a working settlement built downward through generations of black-stone excavation. Its streets occupy broad terraces cut into the quarry walls, with switchback roads, ember-lit lifts, carved records, canvas ash screens, and warm communal interiors contrasting against the dark stone.

The town is a required second-tier region containing the Ashen Crest and Darkness Resonance Seal. It connects directly to Emberglen, Cinderrail Foundry, and the optional wetland town of Lanternfen. Confluence controls when the outer network opens, but there is no direct road from Confluence or Stormbreak to Gloam.

Gloam must not be presented as evil, cursed, or permanently miserable because it specializes in Darkness cards. It is a practical town with loud meals, experienced workers, teasing children, and strict safety customs. Solemnity belongs to the memorial work and the honest accounting of loss, not every ordinary conversation.

## Navigation principles

- **Slatecross Terrace** is the central landmark and first-arrival hub. The inn, card shop, route board, quarry office, and paths toward the arena and Ashvault are visible from it.
- The town uses three main elevations: the upper road and working quarry, the civic and residential terraces, and the lower memorial district. Every required destination is reachable by broad ramps or switchbacks.
- Ore lifts and stair towers provide shortcuts, but essential navigation must never depend on waiting for machinery or using a one-way lift.
- Main paths should be at least two tiles wide. Narrow ledges may contain optional treasure or viewpoints but never a required NPC, shop, or story interaction.
- Colored lantern cages identify regional roads: gold for Emberglen, orange for Cinderrail, and green for Lanternfen.
- Memorial lanterns use steady violet flames. Active quarry warnings use blinking red lamps. Players should be able to distinguish remembrance from danger immediately.
- Black stone must not make the map visually unreadable. Walkable surfaces use pale aggregate edges, brass studs, canvas awnings, and warm window light to separate them from walls and pits.
- Controlled blasts, lifts, furnaces, and falling gravel may animate in background spaces, but the player is never asked to cross a timed industrial hazard.
- Important buildings need distinct silhouettes: the Quarry Office clock, the Record House's carved facade, the arena's split brazier towers, and the Ashvault's lantern-lined arch.

## Central civic terrace

### Slatecross Terrace

Slatecross is a wide public platform where the three regional roads meet the quarry's internal ramps. Its paving is made from rejected stone blocks whose flaws are deliberately left visible and labeled. The practice reflects a local belief that understanding a failure makes it useful.

The terrace contains:

- A relief map showing the quarry's three elevations and all regional exits
- Three route lanterns with differently colored metal cages
- A shift bell and mechanical clock linked to the lift schedule
- A circular casual-duel floor outlined in pale stone
- Covered benches beneath adjustable ash screens
- A contract board for hauling, carving, memorial errands, and trainer challenges
- Emergency water barrels and clearly marked first-aid cabinets
- A public chalkboard carrying jokes, meal notices, and lift closures

The player should arrive here on their first visit. A quarry steward explains that the Ashen Crest match and the disappearing memorial echoes are separate responsibilities, allowing the sporting and Hollow objectives to progress alongside one another.

### Quarry Office and Ledger Hall

The Quarry Office occupies a reinforced black-stone building overlooking Slatecross. A square clock tower and brass shift bell make it recognizable from the upper and lower terraces.

Inside are:

- Current excavation maps
- Lift inspection and blast-clearance boards
- Worker assignments and rescue equipment records
- A public accident-reporting counter
- A meeting room for crew representatives
- Archived rubbings taken from older carved shift walls
- A route office coordinating freight with Cinderrail and Emberglen

The town's safety records become important during the Hollow investigation. Missing memorial names and altered load reports share the same blank residue, but the office records must remain useful civic infrastructure rather than merely a clue container.

### Ash and Ember Cardhouse

The regional card shop has a charcoal facade, a red canvas awning, and a lantern sign showing a small flame rising from a graveyard spiral. The interior is bright and conversational, countering visitors' expectation of a gloomy occult shop.

The Cardhouse specializes in:

- Low-cost Darkness creatures intended as controlled sacrifice material
- Chimera and Ghost support
- Graveyard recovery and creature recursion
- Fire speed attackers and targeted removal
- Necrodragon and Phoenix evolution pieces
- Darkness and Fire shield triggers
- A limited selection of Nature graveyard support imported from Lanternfen

The proprietor offers deck advice about creature counts, evolution bait, and the difference between gaining value from a sacrifice and destroying resources without a plan. Mercer remains the broad general merchant; the Cardhouse provides deeper regional archetype support.

### The Shale and Spoon

The Shale and Spoon combines an inn, canteen, and public bath entrance. Its large dining room is one of the loudest places in Gloam. Long tables mix quarry crews, memorial keepers, travelers, and Circuit challengers between shifts.

The building includes:

- A twenty-four-hour canteen serving heavy stews and flatbread
- Guest rooms behind thick sound-insulated walls
- A washroom for quarry dust and ash
- A small performance corner used for dry comic storytelling
- A rematch notice board
- A private room for visiting story characters
- A rear courtyard with laundry lines and equipment racks

Mira and Nyx can use the private room during the Gloam story without displacing local residents from the main hall. After major scenes, NPCs gather here to debate Vey's decision in ordinary language rather than ceremonial speeches.

### Stone Market

The market occupies a sheltered row between Slatecross and the western caravan yard. Most stalls sell ordinary goods rather than memorial objects.

Permanent stalls include:

- Stone cookware and heat-resistant tiles
- Cinderrail tools and replacement lift parts
- Emberglen food and preserved fruit
- Canvas screens, work gloves, lamps, and dust masks
- Carved card cases and black-stone deck weights
- Lanternfen mushrooms and medicinal salves
- A rubbing artist who copies public inscriptions without damaging them

Temporary stalls rotate with open regional roads. The market becomes visibly busier after the outer relay is restored.

## Upper quarry district

### Quarry Overlook

The overlook is a broad fenced platform above the active cut. It gives the player a clear view of terraces, lifts, furnace smoke, and the Ashvault entrance below. A large orientation diagram labels safe public paths and restricted work zones.

The overlook provides:

- The first establishing view of the settlement
- A safe place for introductory dialogue
- Telescopes aimed at lift towers and carved walls
- A memorial plaque for the historic collapse
- Route signs toward Emberglen, the upper quarry rim, and the civic terrace

The player should understand the town's vertical structure from this point without being allowed onto active ledges.

### The Working Cut

The active quarry is a sequence of broad extraction benches east of town. Background workers drill, wedge, mark, and haul stone while the playable route stays behind barriers and pale safety lines.

Visible work areas include:

- A marked cutting face
- Sorting bays for sound and fractured stone
- Cart tracks and loading platforms
- Water sprayers used to control dust
- Tool shelters and emergency alcoves
- A sealed section where unstable resonance has halted excavation

Dema works near a cleared practice yard beside the cut. Her duels take place away from blast markers and lift tracks so her wandering movement cannot obstruct a critical crossing.

### Lift and Hoist Yard

The hoist yard contains two large ore cages, counterweights, repair scaffolds, and a manually operated passenger lift. The machinery should move in the background even when the player uses the adjacent switchback.

The yard supports:

- A shortcut between the upper and civic terraces
- Lift-repair side quests
- Freight animation linking the cut to Stoneworks
- A route toward the upper quarry rim
- Evidence that memorial ash has been transported under false quarry manifests

The passenger lift can become temporarily unavailable during a story scene, but the ramp route must remain open.

### Stoneworks and Carving Sheds

Stoneworks turns quarry output into bridge anchors, furnace linings, memorial tablets, and architectural blocks. Open-sided sheds let the player see carving benches and polishing wheels without entering active work lanes.

The district contains:

- Rough-cut storage
- Precision carving tables
- A public commission counter
- A wall of approved safety symbols
- Memorial tablet inspection racks
- A reclaim yard for pieces that can be recut

One altered tablet has a smooth blank patch where normal carving marks should remain. This offers an early clue without making every missing name supernatural.

### Blast Office and Powder Magazine

The Blast Office is a pale reinforced building separated from the magazine by an empty safety court. Red signal poles display whether a section is clear, preparing, or closed.

The office stores:

- Blast schedules
- Audible and visual warning procedures
- Flag sets carried by Dema
- Vibration records
- Inspection keys
- Emergency route maps

No explosive storage is player-accessible. Story interactions focus on falsified timing records and missing resonance readings rather than handling explosives.

### Upper Quarry Rim and Signal Lantern

A local stone switchback climbs to the northeastern quarry rim without leaving Gloam. A tall lantern mast relays work stoppages, blast warnings, and severe-weather signals to the terraces below.

The rim provides an overlook, an optional trainer position, and a shortcut back to the Working Cut. It is part of the town map rather than a portal or regional road.

## Residential terraces

### Canvas Row

Canvas Row contains sturdy black-stone homes protected by adjustable ash screens. Buildings share walls where possible, but rooflines, window colors, carved lintels, and small courtyards keep the row from appearing uniform.

Housing variations include:

- Quarry-family homes with tool-cleaning vestibules
- Memorial-keeper homes with quiet front rooms
- Compact lift-worker apartments
- Older houses built around preserved wall inscriptions
- Challenger lodging rented during arena events

At least three interiors should be accessible. Their furniture, jokes, toys, and cooking spaces should establish that residents have lives beyond quarry labor and memorial duty.

### Hearth Court

Hearth Court is a shared residential plaza centered on a protected cooking fire. Families eat outside after late shifts, children practice harmless lantern dares, and retired workers supervise casual duels.

The court contains:

- Communal ovens
- Wash basins and dust-beating racks
- A small children's duel circle
- Covered seating
- A toy lantern course
- Notice boards for meal rotations and neighbor assistance

The player can hear the town's dry humor here. Dialogue should puncture the intimidating exterior without mocking genuine grief.

### Bathhouse and Clinic

The bathhouse uses warm water circulated through old quarry channels. An attached clinic treats dust exposure, hearing strain, burns, and ordinary injuries.

Services include:

- Rest and healing
- Safety-equipment inspections
- Dialogue about the historic collapse and modern reforms
- A recovery location after the optional quarry encounter
- A quiet room for people overwhelmed during memorial rites

Medical access remains available during every story state.

### School and Record Classroom

Children learn ordinary subjects alongside quarry signals, inscription care, and the difference between public records and private memories. The classroom opens onto a protected play terrace.

The school supports:

- A rubbing-copy minigame
- Beginner duels
- Dialogue explaining civic titles such as Sister and Brother
- A side quest involving prank memorial lanterns
- Visible evidence that Gloam teaches history without demanding constant solemnity

## Record and memorial district

### Record House

The Record House stands at the start of Namewall Walk. Its facade is built from stones recovered after the historic collapse, each marked with its original location. It is a civic archive, not a temple.

Inside are:

- Shift ledgers and rescue reports
- Name and family indexes
- Spoken-history transcription rooms
- Rubbing tables
- Shelves of sealed personal records
- A consent desk governing who may access private testimony
- A comparison room used during the Hollow investigation

Ivo works here or on Namewall Walk. His trainer position should sit on a broad landing where he cannot block the route to the Ashvault.

### Namewall Walk

Namewall Walk is a descending public avenue carved with worker names, major repairs, accidents, rescues, and changes to safety practice. New records do not hide old mistakes; corrections are added beside the original inscription.

The route includes:

- Lantern niches maintained by Rell
- Benches for families and visitors
- Rain channels protecting older carvings
- Index markers corresponding to the Record House
- Blank patches appearing during the Hollow crisis
- Side alcoves for private memorials

The walk must remain broad and readable. Missing names should appear as restrained pale absences rather than flashing horror effects.

### Memorial Furnaces

Three controlled furnaces burn damaged belongings after their histories have been recorded and their owners consent. The resulting ashes are sealed in labeled vessels or stone compartments.

The complex contains:

- A receiving and consent room
- A speaking chamber where histories are told aloud
- Three separately controlled furnaces
- Cooling tables
- Ash-vessel storage
- Water and Light-resonance safety equipment
- A rear route toward Ash Courtyard

Vey oversees difficult rites here but delegates ordinary work to other civic keepers. The player should never be asked to burn an irreplaceable item without a clear, reversible gameplay presentation.

### Ash Courtyard

The courtyard is a quiet open space between the furnaces, arena, and Ashvault. Sealed ash vessels are placed in black-stone walls beneath individually chosen symbols rather than identical markers.

The courtyard provides:

- A transition from public town spaces to the sanctuary
- Optional remembrance dialogue
- A gathering place after Vey changes her position
- Evidence of stolen or emptied memorial echoes
- A late-game memorial for losses during the Hollow crisis

Its lighting remains warm enough for cards, characters, and inscriptions to be clearly visible.

### Lamplighter Workshop

Rell's workshop stores lantern cages, colored glass, wick tools, and route-marker parts. A test wall demonstrates the difference between memorial violet, warning red, and road colors.

Rell begins near Namewall Walk and later patrols the lower terraces. His workshop can support lamp-repair errands and Darkness/Light deck conversations.

## Ashvault and Darkness sanctuary

### Ashvault Gate

The Ashvault entrance is cut directly into the deepest stable quarry face. A wide arch bears the names of the collapse rescuers, while two black-iron lantern towers make it visible from higher terraces.

The gate includes:

- A keeper's desk
- Visitor and consent rules
- Storage for open flames and unsafe equipment
- A lift-disabled accessible ramp
- A sealed arena entrance
- A checkpoint that opens as the local story progresses

Vey should appear here during major decisions, but routine access is handled by other keepers so the sanctuary does not depend on her standing in one tile.

### Echo Gallery

The upper gallery contains stone compartments holding recorded ash and associated histories. Public memorials have visible summaries; private records show only an owner-approved symbol.

During the crisis:

- Some inscriptions become smooth and blank
- Ash remains physically present while its echo disappears
- Lantern flames lean toward the lower chamber
- Copied voices repeat fragments without context
- Mira can identify deliberate removal while Nyx hears unnatural silence

The gallery should invite investigation without turning private grief into collectible treasure.

### Lower Galleries

Older quarry passages beneath the public vault contain retired record walls, sealed extraction tunnels, and maintenance routes for ventilation and water.

These galleries support:

- A compact exploration sequence
- Graveyard-oriented trainer encounters
- Optional recovery of stolen echo fragments
- Shortcuts unlocked from the sanctuary side
- The discovery of Collector equipment hidden behind a false memorial wall

Navigation uses lantern numbers and wall markings. Darkness should limit atmosphere, not basic readability.

### Darkness Resonance Sanctuary

The sanctuary is a circular chamber surrounding a natural seam of black resonance stone. Five rings of carved names record losses that led to changed choices: rescues, reforms, reconciliations, and rebuilt homes.

The chamber contains:

- The Darkness Seal at its center
- A controlled memorial brazier
- Five record rings
- Keeper platforms
- A graveyard-shaped duel floor used only during stabilization
- Channels carrying resonance toward the Ashvault lanterns

The Curator's apparatus extracts the connection between an event and the choices made afterward. It does not merely steal sad memories. Restoring the Seal requires preserving the ability to learn while rejecting the idea that pain must dictate a person's future.

### Hidden Collector annex

The annex is concealed behind a fabricated continuation of the oldest record wall. Its smooth, contextless inscriptions initially appear respectful but lack tool marks, corrections, and family symbols.

Inside are:

- Echo siphons disguised as ventilation hardware
- Empty ash vessels used as resonance buffers
- Copies of selected testimony stripped of consent markers
- A relay aimed toward the Hollow Archive
- A confrontation space for a Collector-aligned duelist

After the crisis, the machinery is dismantled and documented in the Record House rather than silently destroyed. Gloam preserves evidence without preserving a functioning weapon.

## Ashen Arena district

### Ashvault Memorial Arena

The sanctioned arena occupies a broad amphitheater carved beside the memorial district. Two split brazier towers symbolize destruction followed by a deliberate new use. The arena is civic and competitive rather than funereal.

The arena includes:

- A central duel floor of dark stone with pale graveyard markings
- Tiered seating protected by canvas ash screens
- Registration and deck-check desks
- A memorial-card lending counter
- Two challenger preparation rooms
- A captain's platform for Sister Vey
- Public boards explaining trial rules and card ownership
- A rear connection to the Ashvault Gate

Spectators should remain visible during ordinary matches. The arena becomes quieter during Vey's story confrontation but never turns into an abandoned dungeon.

### Trial of Accounts

Vey's Crest trial gives the player a temporary set of memorial cards and asks them to preserve, trade, or risk those cards across three controlled duels.

The physical route moves through three stations:

1. **The Inventory Table:** The player reads every temporary card and its recorded significance.
2. **The Risk Floor:** The player selects a limited group to use in two preliminary matches.
3. **The Accounting Circle:** The game summarizes what was preserved, spent, recovered, or left unused before the match with Vey.

All temporary cards are returned afterward. Dialogue changes based on the player's decisions, but no choice permanently removes a card from the player's collection.

### Challenger Hall

The hall contains deck tables, lockers, graveyard practice boards, and viewing windows toward the arena. It is used by local trainers and visiting Circuit challengers.

Facilities include:

- Deck-editing tables
- Clear explanations of mandatory sacrifice rules
- A practice scenario beginning with cards in both graveyards
- Rematch registration
- A recovery station before the Crest duel
- Displays honoring clever losses rather than only undefeated records

### Quarry Duel Club

A less formal club meets in a sheltered carving shed above the arena. Its members test sacrifice combinations and argue cheerfully about whether a play was planned or merely lucky.

Rell, Dema, and Ivo can move here for later rematches. The club supports repeatable challenges without crowding the memorial spaces.

## Regional roads

### Northwest road to Emberglen

The Emberglen road climbs from the western caravan yard through older, greener cuts. Gold lantern cages and timber repairs gradually replace black stone. This is Gloam's most direct route back to Mercer and the central hub.

The road remains bidirectional after it opens. Story urgency must never prevent the player from returning to Emberglen for deck building or supplies.

### Eastern freight road to Cinderrail

The Cinderrail road begins at Stoneworks and follows heavy retaining walls toward the foundry descent. Orange lantern cages, rail hardware, and stacks of furnace-lining stone mark the transition.

Freight animation may show carts moving in background lanes. The playable road remains separate and cannot be blocked by a cart cycle.

### Southern lantern road to Lanternfen

The optional Lanternfen road descends from the Ashvault terrace toward wetter ground. Green fungal lamps gradually replace iron memorial lanterns, and stone paving gives way to timber planks.

The side road never lies between the player and the Ashen Crest or Darkness Seal. Lanternfen remains optional while offering graveyard and mana-oriented cards.

## Suggested NPC placement

- **Sister Vey:** Memorial Furnaces initially; Record House, Ashvault Gate, sanctuary, and arena as the story progresses
- **Rell:** Namewall Walk or Lamplighter Workshop; Quarry Duel Club for rematches
- **Dema:** Working Cut practice yard; Blast Office or Duel Club for rematches
- **Ivo:** Record House landing or Ash Courtyard; Challenger Hall for rematches
- **Mira:** Record House comparison room, Echo Gallery, and sanctuary during investigation scenes
- **Nyx:** Ash Courtyard, Lower Galleries, and sanctuary during their personal story scenes
- **Card merchant:** Ash and Ember Cardhouse
- **Innkeeper and cook:** Shale and Spoon common room
- **Quarry steward:** Slatecross map board or Quarry Office
- **Minor residents:** Stone Market, Hearth Court, lift platforms, carving sheds, arena seating, and broad residential walks

Trainer NPCs should occupy plazas, practice yards, or wide landings. They must not wander onto bridge approaches, narrow ledges, lift doors, memorial alcoves, or regional portal tiles.

## Story-state changes

### Before the memorial echoes disappear

- Quarry shifts and lift cycles operate normally
- Namewall lanterns burn with steady violet light
- The arena runs ordinary local matches
- Vey conducts memorial duties without endorsing the Curator
- The oldest lower gallery remains closed for maintenance

### During the Curator's offer

- Smooth blank patches spread across selected inscriptions
- Some ash vessels retain labels but lose their emotional echoes
- Vey restricts access while considering whether silence could be merciful
- Mira and Nyx disagree about preserving every recovered fragment
- Collector equipment redirects sanctuary resonance into the lower galleries
- Residents continue meals, medical care, and essential quarry work

### During sanctuary restoration

- The public lift closes, but switchback routes remain open
- Memorial furnaces pause except for urgent family rites
- The Ashen Arena hosts the three-stage Trial of Accounts
- Lower-gallery lanterns point toward the hidden annex
- Player choices change dialogue but not permanent card ownership

### After restoring the Darkness Seal

- Blank inscriptions are restored with visible repair marks rather than made artificially perfect
- The hidden machinery is displayed as evidence in the Record House
- Vey rejects erasure without pretending every memory must remain constantly present
- Arena rematches and the Quarry Duel Club reopen
- The Cardhouse expands its advanced Darkness/Fire stock
- The Lanternfen road and Cinderrail freight route become busier

### During Act III

- Some carved names fade temporarily, and residents hang handwritten copies beside them
- Lanterns lose color in isolated pockets but essential routes remain lit
- The Record House duplicates critical records with consent
- Vey prepares a memorial for echoes lost in the Archive
- Mira and Nyx may return depending on completed side quests
- Shops, healing, rematches, and every regional exit remain usable

## Implementation notes

- Use a large scrolling exterior map with visible elevation bands. Camera movement and parallax quarry walls should communicate depth without changing the grid rules.
- Keep Slatecross near the navigational center, with at least two surface routes toward the working cut, residential terraces, arena, and memorial district.
- Use ramps and broad switchbacks for all mandatory elevation changes. Lifts are shortcuts and visual set pieces.
- Separate roof and wall tiles for black-stone homes, civic buildings, sheds, and arena structures. Canvas ash screens should be decorative overlays rather than collision-heavy walls.
- Likely dedicated exterior tiles include black-stone wall, slate roof, carved name wall, quarry rubble, pale-edged terrace, ore rail, lift machinery, memorial lantern, ash vessel, furnace, and bridge or ramp pieces.
- Animated lifts, dust sprayers, furnace light, and signal lamps should remain in background or protected spaces.
- Do not cover the whole map in a dark color grade. Local violet lanterns, ember furnaces, pale paths, brass fixtures, and warm windows must preserve readability.
- Interior maps are appropriate for the Record House, Shale and Spoon, Cardhouse, Quarry Office, Memorial Furnaces, Challenger Hall, and Ashvault galleries.
- The Ashvault can use several connected interior maps, but returning to town should never require retracing a long dungeon after every duel.
- Graveyard-oriented choice prompts must use the existing graveyard browser and must never allow an AI player to skip a mandatory sacrifice.
- Story-state changes should swap lanterns, blank inscriptions, barriers, and NPC positions rather than replace the entire exterior map.
- Regional portals remain permanently bidirectional once unlocked. Player starts, portal destinations, NPCs, and shards require distinct walkable tiles in `Lua/World.lua`.
