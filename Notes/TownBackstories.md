# Town Backgrounds

## Purpose

These profiles cover the required settlements and regional hubs on the connected world map. They complement the character profiles in `Notes/CrestHolders.md` and the side settlements in `Notes/OptionalTowns.md`.

Each place should feel as though it existed before the player arrived. A town's history explains its architecture, economy, duel culture, and relationship with neighboring roads. The Hollow crisis disrupts existing local tensions; it does not create every custom or problem in the world.

## Emberglen

**Map role:** Central home town and recurring campaign hub  
**Visual identity:** Warm timber homes, old stone bridges, festival banners, orchards, and five-civilization decorations gathered around a bonfire square  
**Common decks:** Every civilization, usually straightforward local decks built around one cherished signature card

### Founding and history

Emberglen began as a safe stopping place where five old roads crossed beside a grove of ember-colored trees. Caravans camped around the same fire so regularly that permanent kitchens, stables, and workshops gradually replaced the tents. Nobody planned a town; residents simply stopped leaving.

Its central position made Emberglen a neutral meeting place during periods when the five civilizations distrusted one another. The annual Festival of Five Civilizations grew from a practical traders' truce into a joyful civic tradition. Every family contributes food, decorations, or a public duel, and no single civilization is permitted to claim the festival square as its own.

The northern archive road once brought scholars and valuable card shipments through town. Traffic diminished after part of the old archive closed, leaving bridges, storehouses, and guest rooms larger than the local population required. Mercer later used this spare capacity to make Emberglen the region's most dependable general card market.

### Daily life and duel culture

Residents treat dueling as conversation, recreation, and conflict resolution. Most local decks are not optimized tournament lists; they are collections shaped by trades, family gifts, and favorite creatures. This is why Emberglen produces unusually strong signature echoes despite being a small town.

The festival square is the social center, while Aurelia's formal arena handles sanctioned matches. Mercer's shop supplies the surrounding road network, Rook maintains bridges, and the western practice grounds remain available to beginners at almost any hour.

### Campaign role

The Fading attacks Emberglen first because its balanced festival resonance can reveal whether the Curator's blank signal works across all five civilizations. After Act I, the town becomes a place of return rather than a completed tutorial zone. Mercer's expanding stock, upgraded local rivals, Rowan's research, and changing conversations give the player reasons to come home throughout the campaign.

## Glasswater Port

**Map role:** Western trade port, Tidal Crest region, and first stage of the Water investigation

**Visual identity:** Blue-green canals, glass-roofed warehouses, tide gauges, narrow bridges, and ships tied beside administrative offices  
**Common decks:** Water/Darkness information control, hand filtering, discard, bounce, and patient removal

The detailed city, harbor, and building plan is in `Notes/Glasswater Port.md`.

### Founding and history

Glasswater stands where a clear underground river reaches a broad navigable lake. Early Water-civilization engineers lined the canals with pale translucent stone so pilots could see dangerous currents beneath their boats. At sunset the canal floors shine like sheets of colored glass, giving the port its name.

The settlement became wealthy by verifying cargo rather than merely moving it. Glasswater clerks compare manifests, tide records, card registrations, and insurance claims. A generation ago, a contraband scandal nearly destroyed trust in the port authority; Neris Quill earned his reputation by finding the false entries without accusing innocent crews.

The prediction engine began as a public safety project. Duelists voluntarily contributed match records to help harbor patrols model risky decisions under incomplete information. Later administrators expanded it beyond its original consent rules, creating the weakness the Curator exploits. Its public current readings come from a relay connected to the actual Water Seal at Tidevault; the relay is not itself a Resonance Seal.

### Daily life and duel culture

Glasswater residents admire preparation, precise questions, and graceful changes of plan. Public matches often publish partial deck information before a duel, encouraging competitors to decide what they can infer and what must remain uncertain.

Dockside decks favor Water draw and bounce, while investigators add Darkness discard and removal. Traders maintain a strong market for information-oriented cards, but local etiquette considers looking at private cards without permission far worse than an ordinary rules violation.

### Campaign role

The stolen-memory prediction system divides the town between people who value its accuracy and people whose private matches were taken. The player closes the unauthorized system, preserves its voluntary records, and discovers that corrupted outputs have been transmitted upstream to Tidevault. Glasswater resolves the immediate consent crisis and awards the Tidal Crest, but the Water Seal cannot be restored until the late-game Tidevault route opens.

## Rootmaze Commons

**Map role:** Southwestern living settlement, Nature Seal sanctuary, and Verdant Crest region  
**Visual identity:** Homes grown into enormous roots, suspended garden paths, shallow streams, creature shelters, and signposts designed to be moved  
**Common decks:** Nature/Water mana growth, creature families, evolution branches, search, and bounce

The detailed building and district plan is in `Notes/Rootmaze Commons.md`.

### Founding and history

Rootmaze is not one compact town. It is a collection of clearings linked by paths through an ancient grove whose roots slowly change position. The first settlers tried to cut permanent roads and discovered that the forest simply grew across them. Later residents learned to move bridges, homes, and market stalls with the grove instead.

The Commons agreement established that no person could permanently own a path, spring, or creature nesting ground. Families hold stewardship rights only while they keep an area healthy and accessible. Oren's position as Crest Holder grew from this tradition: they were chosen by many communities but do not rule any of them.

Water engineers from Glasswater helped build flexible channels through the roots, creating a long cultural exchange between the two regions. Rootmaze sends herbs, fruit, and creature-care supplies north; Glasswater provides tools that bend without poisoning the soil.

### Daily life and duel culture

Local duels are held in open clearings where summoned creatures have room to move. Residents care deeply about race and evolution lines, treating an evolution creature as the continuation of a relationship rather than a disposable upgrade.

Nature mana growth and Beast Folk are common, while Water cards represent adaptability when the forest changes. Children learn navigation by following creature tracks and are expected to redraw town maps rather than complain that the previous map became wrong.

### Campaign role

The false memories frighten creature echoes into reshaping paths faster than residents can respond. Repairing Rootmaze means reuniting creatures with their bonded duelists and accepting that the kindest route may not be the shortest. Once stabilized, its living roads create southern loops back to Emberglen, Cinderrail, and Honeyreach.

## Stonechant Village

**Map role:** Optional shaman village and Mystery Totem deck center

**Visual identity:** Resonant standing stones, moss-roofed workshops, sounding slats, water bowls, and Totem clearings beneath old trees

**Common decks:** Nature Mystery Totems supported by Dragon, tapping, graveyard, mana, or guardian packages from other civilizations

The detailed village, shop, Totem grove, and quest plan is in `Notes/Stonechant Village.md`.

### Founding and history

Stonechant formed around stones that answer nearby card and creature echoes with stable rhythms. Its shamans are trained listeners, not unquestioned prophets: they compare interpretations publicly and are expected to revise a claim when creatures or other listeners contradict it. Mystery Totems gather there by choice and are treated as neighbors rather than idols or tools.

Oren Canopy studied in the village before becoming Rootmaze's Crest Holder. The relationship explains Oren's Totem deck without making Stonechant another Crest location.

### Daily life and duel culture

Local duelists declare which relationship they are testing before a match. Whispering Totem supports repetition, Techno Totem supports tactical tapping, and Dracodance or Rollicking Totem supports Dragon decks. This produces varied mixed-civilization lists instead of a town full of identical Nature ramp decks.

### Campaign role

Stonechant offers an optional quest about perfect but lifeless counterfeit rhythms. Resolving it expands the player's Totem collection and can recruit a listener for Act III, but awards no Crest, repairs no Seal, and never gates the Archive.

## Cinderrail Foundry

**Map role:** Eastern industrial town, Fire Seal sanctuary, and Forge Crest region  
**Visual identity:** Red brick halls, elevated rail lines, resonance furnaces, bright safety lamps, and metal walkways over controlled channels of heat  
**Common decks:** Fire/Light aggression, protected evolution material, tapping, blockers, and anti-blocker finishers

The detailed town, rail-yard, foundry, and building plan is in `Notes/Cinderrail Foundry.md`.

### Founding and history

Cinderrail formed around a steep ridge that ordinary caravans struggled to cross. Fire specialists cut the first rail passage through the stone, while Light wardens developed the shields and procedures needed to keep the work survivable. The temporary construction camp became permanent when its workshops began supplying every neighboring road.

The town is owned cooperatively by its rail and foundry crews. Major decisions are argued on the work floor, tested at small scale, and then put to a public vote. Brassa Vale rose from apprentice riveter to chief railwright because she could explain failures without humiliating the person who made them.

Its duel club began as an after-shift recreation league. Crews used matches to decide who received scarce practice time on new equipment, then pooled their winnings to pay entry fees for younger workers. The club's popularity eventually justified a sanctioned Forge Crest arena.

### Daily life and duel culture

Cinderrail respects action, but reckless speed is considered amateurish. The admired duelist is the one who attacks early because they prepared correctly, not the one who ignores every warning. Fire Humans and Dragons provide pressure while Light blockers preserve valuable workers and evolution material.

Shift whistles structure the day. Markets become busiest when crews change, and public arena matches often run beside repair demonstrations. Clayhearth pottery, Rootmaze lumber, and Emberglen food all pass through its rail yards.

### Campaign role

The Curator's collectors hide resonance-harvesting equipment inside machinery the town cannot easily shut down. Brassa's initial refusal comes from fear that closing the furnaces will isolate several settlements, not indifference to the victims. Separating the rail power from the forced-duel engines restores the Fire Seal and keeps the eastern loop open.

## Dragon Keep

**Map role:** Midpoint castle-town, relay checkpoint, and Confluence Crest region

**Visual identity:** A many-gated stone keep, carved dragon buttresses, layered caravan yards, route banners, and a tall relay mast rising from the central court

**Common decks:** Fire Dragon evolution, Dragonoid and Fire Bird setup, mana denial, plus coherent mixed decks among the caravan wards

The detailed castle, wards, gatehouses, undercroft, Crest route, and building
plan is in `Notes/Dragon Keep.md`.

### Founding and history

Dragon Keep began as a road fortress where several regional routes met above the Confluence, the place where all five resonance currents approach the old road to the Archive. It was never a royal palace. Its wardens protected travelers, settled right-of-way disputes, and opened the gates during dangerous weather. Caravan families built permanent warehouses beneath its walls, innkeepers joined them, and a castle-town grew across the surrounding terraces.

As the frontier became safer, the keep's military purpose faded. Its central watchtower was rebuilt as the Circuit's largest regional relay, while the old muster court became a public Crest-dueling ground. Gatehouses now record road conditions and sanctioned results rather than collecting a lord's toll.

The castle takes its name from the Dragon-linked cards that stabilized an early Confluence surge. Their unusually persistent echoes let the first wardens separate the five currents before they collapsed into blank interference. Keep wardens still use Dragon decks as a deliberate stress test for the relay, and five stone dragons along the outer walls commemorate the original calibration cards.

The town has been rebuilt repeatedly as trade routes changed. A building that once faced the main road may now open onto a quiet courtyard, while yesterday's side gate may become tomorrow's market entrance. Residents view this impermanence as normal and are suspicious of anyone claiming there is only one proper route through the region.

Kestrel Vane chose the Keep after years as a caravan duelist and eventually became its road warden. His habit of rebuilding decks mirrors the town, though the castle's preserved relay records eventually teach him that reinvention without historical understanding leaves old weaknesses intact.

### Daily life and duel culture

Nearly everyone encounters mixed-civilization decks here. Traders bring cards from every direction, but locals mock decks that combine expensive cards without a shared plan. Public deck clinics ask duelists to explain what connects their civilizations before suggesting any changes.

The Fivefold Court is both civic square and Crest venue. The relay mast above it carries match certification, road conditions, weather reports, and emergency messages. Inns and markets remain busy even when no official match is underway, while the old walls provide workshops, archives, barracks for road wardens, and sheltered space for stranded caravans.

### Campaign role

Dragon Keep is the persistent bridge between availability tiers, not a one-way departure point. The Chief Collector corrupts old relay paths and temporarily blocks the outer checkpoints. Kestrel and the player restore them, after which the Keep becomes the easiest route back to Emberglen and outward toward the late regions.

## Tidevault

**Map role:** Late-game headwater town and Water Seal sanctuary

**Visual identity:** Slate roofs, pale spillways, stepped blue pools, pressure gates, archive terraces, and rain-fed channels

**Common decks:** Water control, Liquid People evolution, Cyber Lord selection, Leviathan finishers, and Water/Light or Water/Nature support

The detailed town, basin, sanctuary, and route plan is in `Notes/Tidevault.md`.

### Founding and history

Tidevault surrounds the stable headwater where the Water current records the range of choices still available after a decision. Its keepers built the Water Resonance Seal there centuries before Glasswater existed. Glasswater later received public readings through a downstream relay, which is why outsiders often assumed the Seal sat beneath the port.

### Daily life and duel culture

Residents value models that state their uncertainty and preserve a route for revision. Public decisions are recorded alongside later corrections. Duelists favor patient Water strategies, but local etiquette distrusts a player who gathers information without explaining how it was obtained.

### Campaign role

Glasswater's stolen predictions arrive at Tidevault disguised as healthy calibration evidence. After the Confluence relay opens the outer network, the player brings the recovered consent ledger upstream, restores manual choice at the basin locks, and removes counterfeit archive entries without destroying legitimate voluntary records. Tidevault contains no Crest; its mandatory objective belongs only to the Hollow storyline.

## Stormbreak Plateau

**Map role:** Northeastern highland settlement and Tempest Crest region  
**Visual identity:** Wind bridges, stone shelters, signal towers, bright courier flags, and homes tucked beneath overhanging cliffs  
**Common decks:** Multi-civilization Wave Strikers, formation-based boards, shield triggers, and threshold protection

### Founding and history

Stormbreak has no single protected valley large enough for a traditional town. Its residents occupy several sheltered shelves linked by rope bridges and signal towers. The name refers to the cliffs breaking major storms into smaller, less predictable currents rather than stopping them entirely.

The first permanent residents maintained a courier route between the Archive roads and remote shepherd communities. Every household learned basic weather signs, bridge repair, and emergency signaling. Leadership remains practical: whoever can reconnect the shelters during a storm has authority until the danger passes.

Natasha Gale became Crest Holder after coordinating the plateau through a season when every major bridge failed. The Circuit recognized an existing community role rather than importing an outside arena master.

### Daily life and duel culture

Stormbreak duelists favor cards whose value changes when enough allies assemble. Wave Strikers match a culture built around separate shelters acting as one network. A single creature may look unimportant, just as one signal tower seems small, but the full formation can redirect an entire route.

Courier races, tower maintenance, and forecast contests fill the clear-weather days. Cloudrest provides a calmer place to recover, while Dragon Keep, Mirror, and Tidevault maintain the plateau's strongest trade connections.

### Campaign role

The Curator's false forecasts hide real storms beneath copied courier memories. Restoring the towers reveals that several predictions reconstruct the last journeys of missing people. Natasha's route supplies one of several equally valid approaches to the Hollow finale and remains fully connected to earlier regions afterward.

## Gloam Quarry

**Map role:** Southern quarry settlement, Darkness Seal sanctuary, and Ashen Crest region  
**Visual identity:** Terraced black-stone pits, ember-lit lifts, memorial furnaces, carved name walls, and sturdy homes beneath canvas ash screens  
**Common decks:** Darkness/Fire sacrifice, graveyard recursion, removal, speed attackers, and Necrodragon evolutions

The detailed quarry, memorial district, arena, sanctuary, and building plan is in `Notes/Gloam Quarry.md`.

### Founding and history

Gloam Quarry began as a source of dense black stone used for bridge anchors and furnace linings. The stone preserves scratches and heat marks unusually well, leading workers to carve shift records directly into the walls. After a historic collapse, those records allowed rescuers to understand exactly what happened and prevent a second disaster.

The town's memorial tradition grew from that event. Damaged belongings are burned in controlled furnaces, their histories are spoken aloud, and the ashes are sealed beside carved records. “Sister” and “Brother” are civic titles given to trained memorial keepers, not religious ranks.

Sister Vey oversees the Ashvault and its sanctioned arena. The Ashen Crest recognizes deliberate sacrifice and honest accounting of loss rather than celebrating destruction for its own sake.

### Daily life and duel culture

Gloam residents are less gloomy than outsiders expect. Quarry humor is dry, meals are loud, and children dare visitors to walk past harmless memorial lanterns after dark. The seriousness is reserved for records: deliberately erasing a name or concealing an accident is the town's deepest taboo.

Darkness cards place value in the graveyard, while Fire cards turn prepared resources into decisive action. Local duelists distinguish a meaningful sacrifice from waste and expect opponents to make mandatory choices rather than escape their costs.

### Campaign role

The Curator's promise is most persuasive here because Gloam knows memory can preserve pain as well as wisdom. Vey's conflict cannot be resolved by insisting that every memory is automatically good. The player helps the town choose remembrance without submission, restores the Darkness Seal, and keeps its roads to Emberglen, Cinderrail, and Lanternfen open.

## Mirror Arena District

**Map role:** Northern competitive district and Mirror Crest region  
**Visual identity:** Reflective awnings, practice halls, analysis boards, split-color banners, and narrow streets designed around multiple small duel stages  
**Common decks:** Adaptive Water/Light/Darkness control, matchup variants, tapping, discard, and bounce

### Founding and history

Mirror began as a training district outside the capital road, where championship hopefuls hired analysts to study their weaknesses. The district expanded around practice halls, boarding houses, card lenders, and public stages. Its economy depends on competitors staying long enough to rebuild a deck rather than merely passing through.

Early analysts treated every match record as community property, causing scandals when private testing lists reached rivals. Sable Meridian founded the modern Mirror Arena after withdrawing from the Championship and wrote strict consent rules for recording, sharing, and deleting match data.

The name “Mirror” originally mocked the district for copying successful decks. Sable kept it and changed its meaning: a useful mirror reveals something the viewer could not otherwise see, but does not decide what they must become.

### Daily life and duel culture

Mirror residents discuss matchups with the enthusiasm other towns reserve for weather. Aggression, defense, and resource control each have dedicated practice halls, while neutral courtyards encourage players to switch decks between rounds.

The best analysts avoid perfect counters. A respected training deck pressures a weakness while leaving room for the challenger to adapt. Ribbonfair provides comic relief and experimental formats beyond the district's otherwise serious competitive culture.

### Campaign role

The Curator steals private match archives to create hollow replicas of known rivals. Sable destroys the most dangerous records rather than let consent become secondary to preservation. Earning the Mirror Crest contributes an adaptive route into the Archive, and the district remains a late-game testing ground before Crown Gate.

## Sunspire Cloister

**Map role:** Northern Light sanctuary and required Hollow region without a Crest  
**Visual identity:** White stone terraces, gold mirrors, bell towers, shaded libraries, and gardens arranged around a solar observatory  
**Common decks:** Light Guardians, blockers, tapping, shield protection, and disciplined Nature or Water support

### Founding and history

Sunspire was built around an observatory that tracks how Light resonance changes across seasons. Its wardens originally protected travelers on the Archive road, using mirrored signals to warn distant settlements. Over time, the signal school became a cloister devoted to law, mediation, and defensive dueling.

The cloister never sought a Circuit Crest. Its leaders believe sanctuary duties should not depend on defeating a public champion, and the Circuit respects that boundary. Aurelia trained here before moving to Emberglen, carrying its belief that fair rules can create room for courage.

Sunspire's reputation for certainty is both strength and weakness. Generations of careful records make its judgments trusted, but can make wardens slow to accept evidence that contradicts an established account.

### Daily life and duel culture

Life follows bells marking study, maintenance, mediation, and rest. Visitors may join public lessons without taking vows. Guardian decks teach students to protect others first, then identify the moment when defense must become action.

The settlement trades legal expertise, preserved manuscripts, medicinal herbs, and polished signal mirrors. Its road connections to Dragon Keep and Mirror make it a quiet alternative to those crowded competitive centers.

### Campaign role

False memories cause Sunspire's records to accuse innocent wardens with perfect internal consistency. Restoring the Light Seal requires the cloister to value living testimony alongside written proof. Aurelia helps reform its mediation rules, and Sunspire provides a protective approach into the Hollow Archive without awarding a Crest.

## Crown Gate

**Map role:** Final Circuit settlement, Unity Crest arena, and entrance to the Championship territories  
**Visual identity:** Pale stone walls, five colored road bands, formal gardens, competitor lodges, and a broad gate surrounding the final arena  
**Common decks:** All five civilizations, especially Survivors, mana fixing, complementary board effects, and carefully distributed shield triggers

### Founding and history

Crown Gate began as a customs fortress controlling the safest pass into the capital territories. Rather than maintain five competing checkpoints, the surrounding regions agreed to staff one gate together. Its architecture still shows five construction styles joined into a single defensive structure.

The Fivefold Circuit later chose it for final qualification because every challenger already had to pass through. Junior keepers representing each civilization were added to ensure that a finalist understood more than one favored strategy. The town around the gate grew from lodges, registration offices, outfitters, and families serving generations of competitors.

Caelum Rhos transformed the Unity match after his own inconsistent five-civilization championship loss. His Survivor deck demonstrates that cooperation requires structure and retained identity, not a pile of unrelated power cards.

### Daily life and duel culture

Crown Gate is formal but not joyless. Challengers receive tea, route advice, and deck-registration help before anyone asks them to duel. Residents have seen too many nervous competitors to be impressed by boasting, but celebrate sincere improvement loudly.

Deck builders specialize in mana consistency and civilization balance. The junior gauntlet remains educational rather than punitive, and failed challengers may return without repeating unrelated world objectives.

### Campaign role

During the Hollow crisis, Caelum shelters travelers and postpones the official Unity match. He refuses to exploit catastrophe as a qualification test. After the Hollow Deck is defeated, Crown Gate hosts the ninth Crest duel and opens the sporting finale without cutting the player off from Emberglen or the rest of the world.
